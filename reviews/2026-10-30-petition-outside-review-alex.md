## Why you

Picture the reader as someone who has never heard of the project.

## How to read it: two passes
1. **Cold read.** Go straight through once, no notes. Then write down three things: your one-sentence version of what it says; your gut reaction (intrigued, skeptical, bored, uneasy, whatever it really was); and the exact spot where your attention dipped or you first pushed back.
2. **Slow pass.** Go section by section. Note anything unclear, unsupported, overreaching, or that doesn't land. Mark the strong parts too.

## Six questions (short answers are fine)
1. **Comprehension.** Could you restate the core argument after one read? Where did you have to re-read?
2. **Persuasion.** Which claims felt earned, and which felt asserted without support?
3. **Tone.** Does it read as grounded and serious, or does any part drift into grandiose, preachy, or cult-adjacent territory? Be blunt. This is the main reason an outside reader was asked for.
4. **Skeptic test.** Picture the most dismissive smart person you know reading this. Where do they stop, and what's their one-line takedown?
5. **The ask.** Is it clear what the document wants the reader to do by the end? Would you do it?
6. **Trust.** Does anything make you doubt the author's credibility or motives?

**Not needed:** line edits, agreement, or politeness. A polite review that hides a real problem costs more than a harsh one.
