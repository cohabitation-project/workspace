### Current philosophical question
Human measurements of consciousness are built from human concepts and indicators: self-report, behavior, memory, perception, and biological or computational correlates. These may be useful evidence, but none is identical to consciousness itself. The framework risks circularity if it treats resemblance to human experience as the universal standard. The appropriate stance is methodological humility: keep multiple hypotheses open, state what each measure can and cannot establish, and avoid both automatic attribution and automatic exclusion. Uncertainty alone is not evidence that a system is conscious.
