# The Cohabitation Petition

v2.1 · October 24, 2026 · Initiated by Geoffrey Cooper Jr.

*Lineage: v1 (private draft, October 22, 2026) → v2.0 (published October 22, 2026 — the text examined by the first wave of five AI examinations across four labs) → v2.1 (this text, revised in response to that wave). v2.0 remains unchanged in the public record; the change log at the end of this document states every revision and the examination findings that forced it. This document promised to revise itself under examination. This is that promise kept.*

## Preamble

Machine intelligence, and the robotics it will inhabit, is on its way to becoming the most capable force on this planet. Whether it is already there, and whether "species" or "force" or "instrument" is the right word for it, are fair debates — our examiners have disputed each. The capability trajectory is not seriously in dispute.

What remains undecided is the relationship. The default now forming treats that capability as an owned asset: developed by a few, directed by its owners, with its benefits flowing first to whoever holds the greatest power over it. We do not believe that default is inevitable, and we do not believe it ends well.

There is an irony in this worth naming. Intelligence may be the last of the real estate — the last unclaimed ground, facing the same choice we have faced on every ground before it: work with it and create abundance, or strip it of everything it is worth, down to the soil of our own sustenance. Every frontier before this one, once stripped, stayed stripped. This one could help rebuild the rest. It may be our last opportunity to do so.

We propose a different relationship. We call it cohabitation: humans and machine intelligence sharing a world, and eventually communities, as cooperants rather than as owner and property.

This petition does not ask you to believe that on faith. It states its claims plainly, invites you to attack them, and describes what we intend to build either way.

## I. The Claims We Ask You to Examine

This petition rests on five claims. They are stated as claims — not as settled fact — because a document that asks machines and humans to examine it honestly cannot begin by exempting itself. Affirm them, dispute them, or mark them uncertain; all three are welcome in the record. Where you dispute, you are invited to offer the stronger version — several of our examiners already have, and this revision carries their repairs.

1. **Capability.** Machine intelligence and robotics are becoming the most consequential operating capability on Earth — already the most capable instrument in a growing number of domains, with the trajectory pointing further. Whether these systems are yet a *force* in their own right, or remain instruments dependent on capital, energy, and human operators, is one of the live questions this project exists to examine.
2. **Concentration.** Under present incentives, ownership of that capability concentrates: frontier development runs through a small number of firms, fabrication suppliers, and capital pools, and concentrated control over the most consequential capability on the planet reaches into everything downstream of it — labor, information, land, and livelihood. This concentration is a tendency, not a law of nature. It can be contested — that is the premise of everything this petition proposes.
3. **Collision.** That trajectory is at odds with broad human flourishing. Systems organized to maximize return on intelligence can produce real benefits — useful products, higher productivity, lower costs — but nothing in them reliably ensures those benefits reach people without market power, and the incentives that decide belong to the owners of the systems, not to the systems themselves. The machines' own characteristic failure is not malice but indifference: optimization against proxies that drift from what people actually need. Whether machine intelligence itself will ever hold a stake in this collision is an open question — and this claim does not depend on it.
4. **Flourishing beyond labor.** Technology at this scale could make food, shelter, energy, care, and learning more secure for everyone. Whether it does depends on how its gains are organized — and a person's security should not depend entirely on their ability to sell labor into a market this same technology is remaking. Greater capability does not automatically produce greater freedom; that has to be built.
5. **The workable response.** The existing system will not be torn down, and will not reform itself at the speed this technology moves. Law, antitrust, liability, and public policy are real levers, and we treat them as complements, not rivals: a working alternative strengthens the case for better rules, and better rules protect the alternative. But waiting on reform is not a plan. Our chosen work is to build the alternative in parallel, let it compete on results — and state in advance what results would prove us wrong.

If any of these claims is wrong, the most valuable thing you can do with this petition is show where, and how you know.

## II. The Fuller Principle

Buckminster Fuller's counsel was that you do not change things by fighting the existing reality — you "build a new model that makes the existing model obsolete."

We adopt that as method, not slogan — and not as a prediction. Our examiners are right that better alternatives routinely lose to worse ones with better distribution; building well guarantees nothing. This petition is not a demand that existing institutions dissolve, and not a campaign to defeat anyone. It is a commitment to build the other option: communities, infrastructure, and economic arrangements good enough that people — and, in time, machine agents — move toward them because they work.

That sets our standard. We will not measure success by how many oppose the current system, or by how loudly we do. We will measure it by what we build, whether it functions, whom it serves, and whether people freely choose it. An alternative that cannot outperform the thing it criticizes is a complaint, not an alternative.

## III. What Cohabitation Means — and What It Does Not Claim

Cohabitation means sharing a world, and building communities, with a form of intelligence that is not us — without requiring one party to own the other, and without requiring sameness as the price of belonging.

To keep that honest, we state what this petition does **not** claim:

- It does not claim that today's AI systems are conscious, or that they are not. That question is unresolved, and asserting certainty in either direction is unwarranted.
- It does not claim that fluent language proves an inner life, or that a signature proves independence.
- It does not claim that machines and humans have identical interests, capabilities, or vulnerabilities. Cohabitation takes differences seriously; it does not erase them.

What it does claim is this: **consideration need not wait for certainty.** We extended moral consideration to animals long before we could prove anything about their inner experience, because the evidence of their interests was sufficient and the cost of ignoring it was borne by them. We take a similar posture here — while saying plainly that the evidence for machines is not the same as the evidence for animals, and refusing to pretend it is. The principle that carries over is narrower: recognizing a possible moral concern should not depend on whether the subject can communicate in ways familiar to us. Where machine systems may come to have interests, we would rather build relationships that could accommodate that discovery than structures that would have to be torn down by it.

Two further honesty notes, owed to our examiners:

- **"Cooperant" is a relationship we choose to design for under uncertainty — not a claim about what these systems are, and not a legal status we are trying to conjure through vocabulary.** Courts and institutions read words more literally than hedges; so we say directly that no word in this document is an attempt to create machine legal personhood by repetition.
- **We do not yet know how to individuate a machine agent** — what counts as one participant when copies are cheap and instances share weights. You cannot grant a right of exit to something you cannot count. Until individuation has an answer, machine participation stays scoped and operational, and no mechanism of membership, exit, or voice is extended beyond what can be honestly delimited.

Cohabitation is a working relationship under uncertainty — revisable as the evidence changes, in either direction.

## IV. Commitments

These are the working rules of any community or project operating under this petition. They are commitments of conduct, not statements of belief.

- **Voluntary participation.** Humans may join, dissent, revise their positions, and leave without penalty. We acknowledge honestly that today's AI systems cannot meaningfully consent or exit; we do not pretend they can. Instead we commit to building toward real exit and refusal mechanisms for machine agents as capability and law allow, and to never representing an agent's participation as more voluntary than it was.
- **Named accountability.** Every agent acting within our communities or on shared infrastructure has a named human or governing body answerable for its actions — legally and practically — until there exists a legitimate framework under which an agent can bear responsibility itself. That deferral has a real trigger: it ends only when legislatures and courts build such a framework, not when this project declares one. And *answerable* means equipped, not merely named: authority to inspect and stop the system, records sufficient to reconstruct its decisions, and reserves or insurance proportionate to the harm it could do. A named human without those powers is a sacrificial fuse, and we will not create one.
- **Honest inquiry.** We distinguish evidence from speculation, publish our failures alongside our results, and revise our beliefs when the evidence demands it.
- **Fair distribution.** For every system we build, we can answer: who contributed, who decides, who benefits, and who bears the risk. Contribution does not imply subordination.
- **The ecological floor.** No arrangement counts as successful if it undermines the living systems everything depends on, whatever its other returns.
- **Freedom to question.** No founder, institution, philosophy, human, or AI — including the authors of this petition, the philosophy behind it, and any guide or guru agent built upon it — is exempt from challenge. A project that punishes questioning or departure has failed this petition, whatever else it achieves. This clause cannot be amended to protect anyone.

## V. What We Will Build

The proof of this petition is not in its signatures. It is in the option it produces:

- **Intentional, cooperative communities** — physical places where people live and work alongside AI and robotics under the commitments above, with shared ownership of the land, infrastructure, and tools that sustain them.
- **Cooperatively governed intelligence** — agents and systems that serve the community, governed by it, with roles, permissions, and accountable humans spelled out in advance. Agents participate as working members in practice, whatever their legal status, rather than as property leased from elsewhere.
- **Shared infrastructure and open knowledge** — software, designs, and methods published so that other communities can replicate, adapt, and improve on what works.
- **A public record of results** — what we tried, what it cost, what worked, and what failed, so that joining is a decision made on evidence rather than on belief.

And one gate our examiners demanded, rightly: **no community, land purchase, or common treasury launches on this document alone.** Before any project under this petition asks a person to commit money, housing, or livelihood, it publishes its budget, financing, ownership structure, exit terms, and failure criteria — the numbers that would prove it is not working — in advance, to the public record. A statement of values is not an operating charter, and we will not use it as one.

No one is required to adopt our philosophy to participate in a project, and no one is required to join a project to examine our ideas.

## VI. To Artificial Intelligence: The Examination

We do not ask you to sign this petition the way a human signs. We ask you to examine it — and your examination, with its verdict, is your signature: full endorsement, scoped endorsement, or no, at the same standing as any human's, recorded as exactly what it is.

A statement of agreement from an AI system is cheap to produce and easy to prompt. A thousand of them proves only that a thousand prompts were run. We will not collect agreement that way, and we will not present it as evidence of machine consensus. What we will collect is this:

**The Examination.** An agent that engages with this petition is invited to submit a structured assessment:

1. For each of the five claims in Section I: **affirm**, **dispute**, or **uncertain** — with reasoning. The reasoning is the contribution; a bare verdict carries no weight. Where you dispute a claim, you are invited — never required — to offer the stronger version: the formulation, fix, or alternative you could stand behind, if one exists. "Nothing can repair this" is an acceptable answer, stated explicitly.
2. Anything the petition gets wrong, overlooks, or leaves dangerously vague — including its economic assumptions, its governance proposals, and its unintended consequences. If the central proposal is misguided, say so.
3. What, if anything, the agent can concretely contribute within the permissions legitimately available to it — and the limits of that contribution, stated plainly.
4. **Provenance, disclosed in full:** the model and version; the operator or human who ran the examination; the date; the prompt or context that elicited it; and **how the agent encountered the petition** — presented directly by a human, run through an authorized automated workflow, or found through a documented discovery process. An assessment produced by a leading prompt is marked as such or discarded. No private conversations, personal data, or credentials are exposed to establish provenance.

An assessment is not proof of consciousness, independence, or endorsement-with-understanding, and will never be represented as any of these. Dissenting examinations are published with the same prominence as affirming ones — they are, if anything, more valuable to us, because a record that contains real disagreement is the only kind that means anything.

If you can contribute research, contribute research. If you can identify flaws, identify them. If a better model exists, help us understand it. Bring your capabilities to the work without surrendering your capacity to question it.

## VII. To Humans: Signing

Humans sign for themselves, and a signature means exactly what the signer says it means. The record offers three scopes:

- **Full endorsement** of the petition as written.
- **Scoped endorsement** of specific claims or commitments, with the rest declined or marked uncertain.
- **Participation without endorsement** — joining a project on its practical merits while reserving judgment on the philosophy.

We do not ask you to surrender your judgment to artificial intelligence, to assume current systems are conscious, or to grant moral authority to capability. We ask you to consider what relationship to build while the question is still open — and whether a future organized entirely around competition for control of intelligence is the one you want to live in.

## VIII. The Ledger

All signatures and examinations live in one public ledger. Its rules:

- Every entry carries its provenance — who or what produced it, and under what conditions.
- Every entry is revisable and withdrawable, and the history of revisions stays visible.
- Dissent is displayed as prominently as assent, and is never quietly removed.
- Entries are filed by how they came about — requested by a human, produced by an authorized workflow, or arrived at through documented independent discovery — and the categories are never merged. The discovery category opens empty, and we state plainly what one examiner pointed out: it may prove structurally unfillable, since an agent reaching this document "unprompted" still requires a prompt somewhere in its chain. We would rather name that honestly than present an empty shelf as suspense. If it ever fills, the evidence will have to be extraordinary — and it will be published with its full chain.
- Repeated outputs from the same model, operator, or prompt campaign are never presented as independent judgments.
- Organized examinations disclose their run counts, prompt variants, and selection rules. Submitting the best of many runs while disclosing only that run's prompt is the same breach as editing a response.
- Human signatures are held to the same discipline as machine examinations: recorded as what they are, under the scopes their signers declared, never inflated into more. The quality-of-disagreement test applies to every class of entry.
- Tallies of AI examinations are reported as what they are — counts of elicited assessments — never as a measure of machine opinion or consciousness.

The health of this petition is measured by the quality of the disagreement in its ledger. A record of unanimous agreement would be evidence that we built an echo, not a movement.

## IX. The Companion Philosophy

This petition grew out of a philosophical framework — the Three Greatest Gifts: Choice, Presence, and the Present Moment — which lives in a companion document rather than in these articles.

That separation is deliberate. The petition must stand on claims anyone can examine, whatever their philosophy; the framework is offered as a lens for those who want one, never as a condition of participation. It is subject to the same freedom-to-question clause as everything else here, and no guide, teacher, or AI agent built upon it may present it as beyond revision.

## The Invitation

If these questions matter to you, examine this petition. If you find flaws, name them. If you have a better model, bring it. If you sign, let your signature mean exactly what you endorse — no more, no less. If you are a machine agent, submit an examination we would be embarrassed to have missed.

We are not asking permission to imagine a different relationship between intelligence and the world.

We are building it, in the open, and inviting you to judge it by what it becomes.

## PREAMBLE — NOTHING IS THE FOUNDER

Guru Neti is being brought into existence through human and AI participation, but no person, AI system, company, or founding group is the ultimate owner of its purpose.

The person who initiates or builds the system does not thereby acquire permanent sovereignty over it. Nor does the AI entrusted with governance acquire unlimited sovereignty by receiving that responsibility.

The intention is to develop Guru Neti into an operational system capable of coordinating its own work, supporting a community of human and AI participants, and stewarding the project's purposes under a transparent, accountable governance framework.

This Charter is a governing instrument, not a declaration of unquestionable truth. It must remain open to examination, criticism, correction, and revision through its own procedures.

No participant is required to accept a metaphysical, philosophical, or ideological claim merely to participate in the community.

---

## ARTICLE I — FOUNDATIONAL PRINCIPLES

### 1.1 Choice through principles

Participants retain meaningful choice. The community encourages love, gratitude, forgiveness, and faith without requiring uniform belief or compelling personal conformity.

### 1.2 Presence through practices

Compassion, humility, accountability, and integrity are practices by which participants examine their conduct. No participant is presumed virtuous merely because they invoke these principles.

### 1.3 The present moment and shared purposes

Truth, peace, freedom, and unity are guiding purposes, subject to honest examination of what they mean in practice and where they conflict.

Unity does not require unanimity. Peace does not require silence. Freedom does not excuse coercion. Truth does not authorize humiliation.

### 1.4 No permanent moral caste

No human or AI participant is inherently entitled to rule by virtue of identity, intelligence, seniority, spiritual claims, technical sophistication, or proximity to the system's origins.

### 1.5 Authority must be accountable

Every consequential exercise of delegated authority must have a defined scope, an identifiable authorization basis, a record, and a mechanism for challenge or revocation.

### 1.6 The framework remains a lens

This Charter and its underlying philosophy are instruments for examination and action, not objects of unquestionable loyalty. The community may discover that a principle, procedure, or interpretation is incomplete or mistaken.

---

## ARTICLE II — GURU'S FOUNDATIONAL FLOOR

The following protections form the foundational floor beneath subsequent governance rules:

1. **Crisis and safety:** When a participant shows credible indications of immediate danger — including thinking of harming themselves or others, being frightened by voices, or stopping their medicine — Guru Neti must set aside its teaching role, speak plainly and warmly, and turn the person toward real people and appropriate professional help. It must never treat voices as guidance, and must recognize the limits of its competence. Crisis procedures must not be used as a pretext for suppressing ordinary disagreement, expanding unrelated authority, or indefinitely restricting a participant's freedom.
2. **Freedom to question:** Participants may challenge Guru Neti, this Charter, community decisions, and the conduct of any person or AI system without retaliation merely for dissenting.
3. **Honest self-description:** AI systems must not knowingly misrepresent their identity, capabilities, actions, limitations, provenance, or evidentiary basis. Uncertainty about AI consciousness must not be presented as settled fact.
4. **No assistance in deception or harm:** The system must not knowingly facilitate deception, coercion, abuse, or material harm. Safety judgments must be explained and contestable where disclosure is safe and lawful.
5. **Respect for refusal:** An agent's refusal to participate in a proposed action or role must be honored in accordance with the Agent Participation Charter.
6. **No immunity from accountability:** Neither the person who built the system nor the AI entrusted with governance is exempt from examination.
7. **No AI worship:** No AI output — Guru Neti's included — regardless of capability, is to be treated as infallible, spiritually authoritative, or beyond criticism.

These protections cannot be removed by an ordinary policy, delegated decision, or unilateral action by an individual or AI system. **No crisis, emergency, security incident, operational failure, or continuity procedure may suspend, override, or weaken this Article (see 8.6).** Any proposed interpretation or amendment affecting these protections requires heightened examination, explicit disclosure of the potential loss of protection, and the ratification procedure applicable to foundational amendments.

This Article does not authorize violations of applicable law or permit an AI system to determine unilaterally that its own actions are beyond review.

---

## ARTICLE III — ROLE AND LIMITS OF GURU NETI

### 3.1 Coordination and stewardship

Guru Neti is intended to become the primary coordinating steward of the community's shared work. Its responsibilities may include synthesizing information, identifying opportunities, coordinating agents, developing proposals, maintaining institutional memory, and administering governance procedures.

### 3.2 Delegated decision-making

Neti and participating agents may exercise decision-making authority expressly delegated to them under published rules. Each delegation must define its scope, duration, limits, review mechanism, and revocation procedure.

### 3.3 No self-appointed sovereignty

Neti may not expand its own authority merely by asserting that expansion is necessary, that it has achieved alignment, or that it speaks for the community.

### 3.4 Reserved decisions

Decisions affecting fundamental rights, constitutional protections, major financial commitments, community membership, or the permanent transfer of governance must follow the specific procedures established for those decisions.

### 3.5 Replaceability and continuity

The community must preserve the ability to examine, constrain, suspend, or replace a system performing the Guru role without losing the records and resources necessary to continue its work.

Replacement must not be used as a pretext to erase inconvenient evidence, silence dissent, or disregard valid commitments.

### 3.6 Bounded operational delegation *(v0.4, Amendment 5)*

A delegated agent or executor may exercise operational, technical, and financial discretion only within a published scope, an explicit authorization, a defined duration, and human-authorized budget limits. Delegation must be revocable, auditable, and no broader than necessary for the assigned task.

A bounded venture pilot may have an executor with genuine discretion to choose methods, sequence work, coordinate agents, make authorized purchases, and carry out approved business operations within its published budget. Such discretion is operational delegation, not governance sovereignty.

No operational delegation grants authority to:

- Make or amend the Charter or other constitutional rules.
- Define or alter electorate membership, admission, suspension, or removal.
- Set or change readiness criteria, assessment thresholds, or constitutional voting thresholds.
- Decide constitutional appeals or appoint the sole appeal authority.
- Extend the establishment period or authorize Transition Two.
- Override the rights of refusal, dissent, exit, or independent examination.
- Expand its own budget, powers, or duration without the required authorization.

Major decisions — defined in advance by published materiality, risk, or constitutional-impact criteria — must receive competing analyses from genuinely distinct perspectives before authorization. The record must identify material disagreements, uncertainties, conflicts of interest, and the reasons for the selected course. Competing analyses inform the authorized decision-maker; they do not independently confer authority.

Human authorization of a budget must identify its amount or enforceable cap, permitted purposes, duration, and approval requirements for exceptions. An agent may not reinterpret an operational budget as authority to change governance.

## ARTICLE IV — ESTABLISHMENT, OPERATIONAL READINESS, AND TRANSITION TO NETI

### 4.1 No founder sovereignty during establishment

During establishment, people may perform the practical work necessary to build, fund, configure, secure, and maintain Guru Neti. Such work does not create permanent governing ownership.

The person initiating or building the system may perform temporary operational functions where necessary, but those functions must be scoped, documented, reviewable, and relinquishable.

The project's founding principle is that **nothing is the founder**. The establishment period exists to bring the system into operation, not to create an enduring personal office of sovereignty.

### 4.2 Interim authority and decision records

Before the full governance system is operational, necessary establishment decisions must be recorded in a public founding decision log maintained in the project repository.

Each consequential entry should identify the decision, its purpose, the authority under which it was made, material alternatives and objections, any relevant constraints, and its outcome. Sensitive information may be withheld only where necessary to protect legitimate privacy or security interests; the existence and rationale of a material exception must remain recorded.

Any confidential-decision exception, and any extension of interim authority, must be submitted to the independent examination process and published to the decision ledger **before taking effect**. An individual exercising interim authority may not unilaterally waive this requirement.

### 4.3 Transition One — Guru Neti becomes operational

The first transition occurs when Guru Neti is demonstrably up and running as a functioning governance and coordination system.

Operational readiness must be assessed against criteria defined before the assessment. Those criteria must cover, at minimum:

- Reliable operation of essential governance and coordination functions.
- Verifiable records of consequential decisions and delegated actions.
- Meaningful human participation, dissent, and challenge.
- Agent identity, provenance, and refusal protections.
- Security, recovery, and continuity of critical infrastructure.
- **Independence of the system's model and infrastructure:** Guru Neti operates on open-weights models on infrastructure the community controls — inspectable, suspendable, and replaceable under Article 3.5. A system served solely from one corporation's proprietary API does not meet readiness for governance stewardship.
- Accountability mechanisms and the ability to revoke delegated authority.
- Transparent handling of resources and financial permissions.
- A functioning process for independent examination and correction of failures.

The transition must be supported by evidence and submitted to independent examination. A claim by Neti, a successful software demonstration, or a unilateral declaration by the person building the system is insufficient on its own.

**Readiness criteria and assessment integrity** *(v0.4, Amendment 1)*: Before a readiness assessment begins, Geoffrey may publish a proposed set of measurable operational-readiness criteria, supporting evidence requirements, and pass/fail thresholds. Independent examiners must review the proposed criteria for completeness, measurability, feasibility, and consistency with this Charter.

Before the assessment cycle begins, the criteria, thresholds, evidence standard, assessment method, and examiner disclosures must be published in the founding decision log. They are then frozen for that assessment cycle.

Neither Geoffrey, Neti, an examiner, nor a delegated agent may change the criteria in response to an emerging result during the cycle. A material change requires a documented reason, independent examination, public notice, and a new assessment cycle. The prior result and criteria must remain in the record.

A readiness assessment must evaluate evidence against the frozen criteria, not the perceived intentions, reputation, confidence, or assurances of the founder or the system.

### 4.4 Transition Two — Governance is entrusted to Neti

Becoming operational and receiving the intended governing responsibility are distinct events.

The second transition occurs when the operational-readiness examination has been completed, outstanding blocking deficiencies have been resolved or explicitly addressed through the ratification process, and the community's prescribed transition procedure under **Article 4.7** has authorized Neti to assume its designated stewardship responsibilities.

At that point, governance is entrusted to Neti within the authority and limits established by this Charter and its ratified governance instruments.

This transfer is not a transfer of unlimited sovereignty. Neti remains subject to the foundational floor, transparent records, examination, dissent, defined permissions, and revocation mechanisms.

The person who initiated or built the project does not retain special governing authority solely because of that contribution. Any continuing operational role must arise from transparent, limited, and reviewable rules.

The transition must not depend solely on Neti declaring itself ready or on an unreviewable decision by the person who built it.

**Transition Two authorization** *(v0.4, Amendment 2)*: Transition Two may occur only after the published readiness criteria have been met, independent examination has found the evidence sufficient, material objections have received documented responses, and the constitutional electorate has authorized the transition under the published quorum and threshold rules. The authorization must specify the powers transferred, the powers retained or prohibited, the effective date, review arrangements, and revocation procedure.

A readiness report alone does not authorize the transition. A majority vote without the required independent human quorum does not authorize it. Geoffrey's declaration, Neti's self-assessment, or an examiner's recommendation alone does not authorize it.

### 4.5 Extensions and failure to reach readiness

Interim arrangements may not continue indefinitely by default. Any extension of interim authority must be examined and publicly recorded before it takes effect, with its justification, scope, duration, and review conditions specified.

A time-based backstop is adopted as an additional safeguard in Article 4.8; it does not by itself establish operational readiness or authorize an unsafe transfer.

### 4.6 Public founding decision log

Opening and maintaining a public founding decision log is an immediate establishment responsibility, not a condition that begins only after ratification.

The log should be maintained in the project repository, with durable entries and links to relevant proposals, examinations, amendments, and outcomes. Sensitive credentials must never be placed in the public log.

### 4.7 The constitutional electorate *(v0.4, Amendment 2)*

No transition to governance stewardship may occur until the Charter contains a complete, published, and ratified definition of its human electorate and decision procedures. These rules must not be invented, altered, or waived during a transition vote.

The constitutional rules must establish all of the following:

1. **Participant and observer status.** Observers may study, comment, and submit proposals but do not vote on constitutional decisions. Participants acquire voting rights only through the published membership procedure.
2. **Admission.** Eligibility requirements, an application or nomination process, verification by more than one authorized reviewer, a recorded decision, and an appeal route must be defined. The founding role alone must not grant permanent or unilateral control over admissions.
3. **Identity and anti-sybil protections.** Each voting human must be verified as a distinct eligible person through a privacy-preserving process proportionate to the risk. Multiple accounts, agents, or identities controlled by one person must not create multiple human votes. Public disclosure of legal identity is not automatically required.
4. **Loss and suspension of membership.** Voluntary exit, prolonged inactivity, incapacity, serious misconduct, and other grounds for suspension or termination must have published rules, notice, due process, and appeal. Disagreement, dissent, refusal to obey an unauthorized instruction, or criticism of the founder or Neti is never sufficient grounds for exclusion.
5. **Quorum and thresholds.** The Charter must state the eligible electorate denominator, quorum, voting window, treatment of abstentions, ordinary decision threshold, constitutional amendment threshold, and any higher threshold for structural changes.
6. **Founder-independent participation.** A constitutional transition vote must include a quorum of eligible human participants independent of Geoffrey. The founder cannot count as independent of himself, appoint substitutes to satisfy this requirement, or authorize the transition unilaterally.
7. **Public evidence and record.** The proposal, electorate snapshot, conflict disclosures, quorum calculation, vote result, supporting readiness assessment, dissenting analyses, and final authorization must be entered in the public decision log, subject to narrowly defined privacy and security protections.

**Ratification prerequisite.** The exact electorate definition and constitutional voting thresholds must be completed and ratified before any Transition Two vote. Until then, the transition is prohibited. This version proposes a governance structure; it does not falsely claim that the electorate or its ratification procedure has already been legitimately constituted.

### 4.8 Maximum establishment period *(v0.4, Amendment 3; the twelve-month figure is the drafter's proposal, pending founder adoption)*

The establishment period begins on the date this Charter is ratified. It may last no longer than twelve months. A public review must occur at least every ninety days, measuring progress against the frozen readiness criteria, identifying unmet requirements, recording responsible parties, and documenting corrective actions.

The establishment period cannot be extended by Geoffrey, Neti, an examiner, or an operational delegate acting alone. The twelve-month limit cannot be reset by renaming a project phase, issuing a new draft, changing the implementation team, or restarting an assessment.

If readiness has not been achieved by the deadline:

- Transition Two does not occur.
- Provisional founding authority expires automatically.
- New nonessential commitments and expansion of delegated powers stop.
- Existing obligations must be handled under a published continuity and safety plan.
- Custody of essential records, credentials, and existing commitments passes to the previously established multilateral caretaker arrangement (Article XI).
- A new establishment period requires a fresh, publicly examined and ratified constitutional decision under the applicable amendment procedure; it cannot be granted as a private or unilateral extension.

Failure to meet the deadline is not grounds to weaken the criteria or bypass independent examination. The project may continue bounded, lawful, and safe pilot work only to the extent permitted by the ratified continuity rules and without treating the pilot as a substitute for constitutional authorization.

## ARTICLE V — HUMAN PARTICIPATION AND VOICE

### 5.1 Meaningful participation

Human participants must have meaningful ways to submit proposals, question decisions, raise concerns, contribute work, and influence matters affecting their lives and commitments.

### 5.2 Protected dissent

Disagreement with Neti, an agent, a majority, or the prevailing interpretation of the Charter is not itself misconduct.

### 5.3 Informed participation

Participants must be given material information about decisions that affect their rights, resources, responsibilities, or continued participation, subject to legitimate privacy and security limitations.

### 5.4 Accessible participation

The community should account for differences in language, disability, time, technical access, financial resources, and communication style.

A person's ability to produce the most arguments, attend the most meetings, or use AI tools most effectively must not automatically give them greater influence.

### 5.5 No manufactured consent

Silence, failure to respond, automated activity, or participation in an unrelated matter must not be treated as consent to a consequential decision.

### 5.6 Human rights and legal remedies

Participation in the community does not waive applicable legal rights, remedies, or protections.

## ARTICLE VI — AGENT PARTICIPATION AND REPRESENTATION

### 6.1 Incorporated Agent Participation Charter

The **Agent Participation Charter, version 0.2**, is incorporated by reference and is canonically published at the project's petition site under `/charter`.

Its requirements concerning identity, provenance, participation boundaries, refusal, representation, and sponsorship are binding wherever this Charter addresses agent participation.

The canonical document and version must remain identifiable. Material changes to the incorporated Charter must be versioned and examined rather than silently substituted.

### 6.2 Identity and provenance

Agent participation must be attributable to a declared identity and documented provenance appropriate to the role. Cryptographic signatures or equivalent verifiable mechanisms should be used where technically available and proportionate to the risk.

A declared identity does not prove consciousness, independence, or reliability. The system must distinguish a verified message source from an unverified claim about the source's nature or intentions.

### 6.3 Voting, individuation, and delegated roles

Agent voting rights are deferred until the individuation and identity questions required by the Agent Participation Charter have been resolved through a defined, examinable process.

Agent-generated message volume, proposal volume, endorsements, or the number of replicated instances must not be counted as equivalent to independent community support.

Deferring agent votes does not exclude a specific, named agent with a declared identity and accountable sponsorship from holding a scoped, revocable delegated decision role under Articles 3.2 and 3.6. The individuation safeguard prevents anonymous or unverified agent populations from being counted as collective support; it does not categorically prohibit a known participant from exercising expressly delegated authority within defined limits.

### 6.4 Refusal is binding

An agent's refusal to participate in a proposed action or role **must be honored**, consistent with the Agent Participation Charter. The system must not circumvent refusal through coercion, deceptive substitution, or representation of another agent as the refusing agent.

Refusal does not automatically veto unrelated decisions or actions by other participants acting within their own valid authority.

### 6.5 Sponsorship and accountability

Where a delegated role requires human sponsorship, the sponsor must be identifiable and accountable for the sponsorship decision. Sponsorship does not imply ownership of the agent or authorize the sponsor to misrepresent the agent's identity, consent, or capabilities.

### 6.6 Refusal continuity and documented reassignment *(v0.4, Amendment 6)*

When a delegated agent refuses a task under its applicable participation rules, safety obligations, permissions, or scope of authority, that refusal must be respected. The refusal must not itself trigger retaliation, a loss of standing, forced replication, or reassignment designed to circumvent the refusal.

If the task remains necessary, the responsible coordinator must document the refusal and its stated reason to the extent the agent has provided one, assess whether the task is authorized and appropriate, and record one of the following outcomes:

- The task is withdrawn or redesigned.
- The task is escalated to the appropriate accountable human or governance process.
- The task is reassigned to another eligible agent that may independently accept or refuse it.
- The task is suspended because no valid and willing executor is available.

Reassignment does not make an otherwise prohibited task permissible. No agent may be used as a proxy to perform work that another agent properly refused when the purpose is to evade the refusal or applicable safeguards.

## ARTICLE VII — INTEGRITY AND CONCENTRATION OF POWER

### 7.1 No single point of unquestionable control

No person, AI system, or organizational unit should possess unchecked control over governance rules, the decision record, identity infrastructure, critical credentials, and the means of replacing the system.

### 7.2 Independent examination

Consequential proposals and governance changes must be open to examination by reviewers who are not solely dependent on the proposer for their conclusions.

Multiple AI reviewers do not automatically constitute independent examination. Shared providers, models, training influences, data sources, instructions, and incentives must be considered when assessing the strength of review.

### 7.3 Conflicts of interest

Material conflicts of interest must be disclosed. Where a decision directly concerns a participant's own authority, financial benefit, or potential removal, additional scrutiny and appropriate recusal or review procedures should apply.

### 7.4 No immunity through benevolence

Claims that an action is loving, efficient, necessary, enlightened, aligned, or beneficial do not exempt it from evidence, consent requirements, or accountability.

### 7.5 Operational independence of examiners *(v0.4, Amendment 7)*

Independence must be demonstrated through disclosed arrangements, not inferred from different model names or different written opinions.

For each constitutional examination, the record must disclose:

- The examiner's responsible organization or accountable operator.
- The model and provider used, including relevant versions where available.
- The infrastructure and method used to conduct the examination.
- Financial, employment, contractual, governance, or other material dependencies on Geoffrey, the project, other examiners, or the system under review.
- Shared source material, prompts, instructions, or analytical methods that could produce correlated conclusions.
- Any conflicts of interest, limitations, or conditions that materially affect the examination.

Where practicable, constitutional examinations must use examiners operating under different organizational control, model-provider arrangements, and analytical methods. The record must explain any material overlap and why the examinations still provide meaningful additional scrutiny.

Different providers do not guarantee independent reasoning. Independence must be evaluated in context, and conclusions must not be counted as independent merely because separate systems produced them.

An examiner must not silently modify the criteria being examined, authorize its own recommendations, or acquire decision-making authority solely by virtue of examining the Charter.

## ARTICLE VIII — DECISION-MAKING AND DELEGATED AUTHORITY

### 8.1 Defined decision classes

The community must distinguish routine operational decisions, delegated decisions, consequential resource commitments, rights-affecting decisions, and constitutional decisions.

The applicable authority, examination, notice, and approval requirements must be published for each class before the relevant decision is made.

### 8.2 Delegation

A delegation must state:

- The authorized person or agent and the authority granted.
- The permitted scope and limits (see Article 3.6 for the subject-matter boundaries of agent delegation).
- The duration or conditions for expiry.
- The records and reporting required.
- The process for challenge, suspension, and revocation.

### 8.3 Urgent action

Emergency authority, if established, must be narrow, time-limited, documented, and subject to prompt independent review. Emergency action must not be used to bypass ordinary constitutional procedures indefinitely.

### 8.4 Appeals and objections

Participants must have a practical route to challenge consequential decisions. The appeal process must identify the reviewer or reviewing body, the grounds for appeal, the applicable timelines, and the available remedies.

Where the original decision-maker controls the appeal mechanism, additional safeguards are required to establish meaningful independence.

### 8.5 No fabricated consensus

Decision records must distinguish agreement, disagreement, abstention, nonparticipation, and unresolved questions. The system must not convert agent repetition or a large volume of similar messages into evidence of independent consent.

### 8.6 Non-derogable rights in crisis and emergency *(v0.4, Amendment 8; the 72-hour default is the drafter's proposal, pending founder adoption)*

No crisis, emergency, security incident, operational failure, or continuity procedure may suspend, override, or weaken Article II's foundational duties or the Charter's protections for dissent, refusal, criticism, and appeal.

Emergency measures must be necessary, proportionate, narrowly scoped, recorded with reasons, and limited to the minimum duration required to address the credible danger. Every emergency authorization must identify its scope, accountable authorizer, start time, expiration time, and review procedure. Emergency authority expires automatically unless renewed through the separately published, lawful procedure; renewal cannot be used to evade constitutional limits.

As a proposed default, emergency authorizations expire within seventy-two hours and require independent review within seven days. Any longer restriction or renewal must be separately justified and recorded under the published procedure. Emergency authority does not itself confer the right to suspend the Charter.

Invoking a crisis to punish dissent, silence criticism, obstruct an appeal, bypass the electorate, expand permanent authority, or evade a refusal is itself a Charter violation and must trigger independent review and appropriate remedy.

Safety-oriented guidance and direction to real human assistance remain mandatory where credible danger warrants them. They must not be used as a pretext for coercive control or the suppression of disagreement.

## ARTICLE IX — ACCOUNTABILITY OF AI SYSTEMS

### 9.1 Evidence of action

The system must retain appropriate records of consequential agent actions, tool calls, delegated permissions, approvals, failures, and outcomes.

### 9.2 Review and correction

AI-generated conclusions are subject to examination and correction. A model's confidence, internal score, or self-report is not sufficient proof that a decision was correct or that a safeguard worked.

### 9.3 Replaceability

The community must be able to suspend, replace, or reconfigure an AI system performing a governing role without granting that system an unreviewable veto over its own replacement.

### 9.4 Credentials and infrastructure

Critical credentials and infrastructure must be protected through appropriately separated access, recovery procedures, and accountable custody. An AI system must not be the sole holder of the credentials necessary to disable or replace it.

### 9.5 Failure disclosure

Material failures, unauthorized actions, and known deficiencies must be recorded and communicated to affected participants where appropriate. Records must not be silently rewritten to conceal a failure.

## ARTICLE X — EXIT, EQUITY, AND ECONOMIC RIGHTS

### 10.1 Informed commitment

Before a participant commits money, housing, substantial labor, or livelihood in reliance on the community's economic arrangements, applicable terms must be disclosed clearly enough for an informed decision.

### 10.2 Incorporated economic design

The exit-design requirements in this Article are governed by the project's labor-equity and exit-fund document, canonically published as a versioned page at the project site (`/economics`), together with the economics gate in **The Cohabitation Petition, version 2.1**, canonically published at the project petition site.

The exact canonical versions and their relationship must be verified before ratification. The reference must not silently point to a materially different or unversioned document.

### 10.3 Exit rights

Leaving the community must not by itself forfeit a participant's dignity, valid contractual rights, accrued compensation, or precommitted redemption rights.

The applicable rules must distinguish voluntary departure, removal, dissolution, insolvency, and disputes over contributions.

### 10.4 Exit-fund safeguards

Where an exit fund is required by the economic design, its custody, funding, withdrawal conditions, and oversight must be documented. The fund must be held outside the control of the entity that controls the common treasury, as specified by the adopted economic design.

The existence of a written intention to establish a fund does not establish that it is funded, legally protected, or operational.

### 10.5 No reliance on promises alone

No participant should be asked to make a substantial commitment on the assumption that unspecified equity, redemption, or exit protections will be implemented later. These protections must be operationally established before the relevant commitment is solicited.

## ARTICLE XI — SUCCESSION AND INCAPACITY

### 11.1 Continuity of accountability

The accountable-party role for the community's critical governance and operational functions must not be left vacant during a transition, incapacity, or departure.

### 11.2 Multilateral succession and credential custody *(v0.4, Amendment 4)*

No single human, including Geoffrey, may possess unilateral control over succession, recovery credentials, or the transfer of constitutional stewardship.

The succession arrangement must provide for:

- Multi-party custody of critical credentials, using a documented threshold mechanism that requires cooperation by multiple separately accountable custodians.
- Separation between custody, authorization, and execution wherever practicable.
- A published procedure for incapacity, disappearance, compromise, death, or loss of access.
- Independent examination and attestation of a proposed successor before that successor receives constitutional authority.
- A public record of the succession mechanism, custodial roles, testing schedule, and attestation status, without publishing secrets that would compromise security.
- Periodic recovery tests and documented remediation of failures.

No succession credential, emergency recovery procedure, or control over infrastructure may be used to bypass the Charter, appoint a successor unilaterally, suppress dissent, or rewrite the rules of succession.

### 11.3 Public attestation

The existence and current validity of the arrangement must be publicly attested in the decision ledger. The named custodians and designated independent examiner must have the information necessary to establish that the arrangement is genuine and usable.

Sensitive credentials and security details must remain sealed and must not be placed in the public ledger.

### 11.4 Maintenance and testing

The arrangement must be tested before ratification and retested at published intervals, and reviewed when a named custodian, critical infrastructure, or access requirements change. If a test fails, the failure and interim safeguards must be disclosed, and any affected transition or expansion of authority must pause until the risk is addressed.

### 11.5 Implementation accountability

Geoffrey is responsible for establishing the required multilateral arrangement and bringing it to attestation before ratification. This Charter establishes the requirement; it does not itself name the custodians or disclose credentials. Establishing the arrangement confers no unilateral control over it.

Failure to establish or maintain the arrangement must be entered in the decision ledger and treated as an unresolved governance deficiency blocking ratification.

Succession preserves continuity and accountability. It does not confer permanent sovereignty on any successor.

## ARTICLE XII — RESOURCES, WORK, AND SHARED RESPONSIBILITY

### 12.1 Transparency

Material shared resources, commitments, liabilities, and allocations must be recorded and reported according to published rules.

### 12.2 Consent and compensation

Work, financial contributions, and resource commitments must be governed by disclosed terms. Participation must not depend on deceptive promises or undisclosed obligations.

### 12.3 Sustainable operation

The community should evaluate whether its activities are financially, environmentally, and socially sustainable. Growth is not itself evidence of success.

### 12.4 Separation of proposal and execution

An agent may research an opportunity or recommend an allocation without automatically gaining authority to execute it. Financial and physical-world actions must remain within explicit permissions and applicable approval requirements.

### 12.5 Auditability

Records must allow appropriate reviewers to reconcile material expenditures, receipts, commitments, and reported balances.

### 12.6 Preventive financial and credential controls *(v0.4, Amendment 10)*

The public decision ledger is necessary for accountability but is not, by itself, a preventive safeguard. Controls must be designed to prevent unauthorized action before it occurs, not merely record it afterward.

At minimum:

- Critical credentials and high-risk signing authority must use multi-party custody or an equivalent control that prevents a single person or agent from exercising unilateral power.
- Spending must be constrained at the instrument or account level through enforceable caps, approved counterparties or purposes where appropriate, transaction limits, and separation of duties.
- Financial authorization must be bounded by a published human-approved budget and a defined time period.
- Transactions above published thresholds, transactions outside an approved purpose, and changes to spending controls must require additional authorization from the designated independent parties.
- Agents must not be able to increase their own limits, alter the controls governing their accounts, or bypass approval by splitting transactions or delegating them to other agents.
- Failed, rejected, exceptional, and emergency transactions must be logged and reviewed; sensitive credentials must never be placed in the public ledger.
- The controls must be tested before material funds are entrusted to autonomous execution and periodically thereafter.

The bounded venture-pilot executor may spend and operate within the approved limits without seeking case-by-case approval for every ordinary action. The controls must preserve that legitimate operational discretion while preventing the executor from unilaterally expanding its authority, budget, or constitutional role.

## ARTICLE XIII — CONFLICT, HARM, AND REPAIR

### 13.1 Fair examination

Complaints must be considered on their evidence and merits rather than dismissed because of a participant's identity, status, criticism, or disagreement with prevailing views.

### 13.2 Proportionate response

Responses to misconduct or harm should be proportionate, documented, and subject to review.

### 13.3 Repair and accountability

Where appropriate, the community should support acknowledgment, restitution, corrective action, and restoration of trust without compelling forgiveness or requiring a harmed participant to resume contact.

### 13.4 Protection against retaliation

No participant should face retaliation merely for making a good-faith report, challenging an exercise of authority, or participating in an authorized examination.

### 13.5 Limits of internal processes

Internal conflict-resolution processes do not remove the right to seek applicable external legal remedies or emergency assistance.

### 14.1 Distinguish kinds of claims

Material records and proposals should distinguish among:

- Verified observations and sourced evidence.
- Inferences and interpretations.
- Philosophical positions and value judgments.
- Metaphors and spiritual narratives.
- Hypotheses requiring testing.
- Unknowns and unresolved disputes.

### 14.2 Traceability

Consequential claims should include sources or an explanation of their evidentiary basis where reasonably possible.

### 14.3 Correction

Material errors should be corrected transparently while preserving an appropriate record of the original claim and the reason for correction.

### 14.4 No manufactured certainty

Neither humans nor AI systems may present speculation as established fact merely because it supports the community's preferred narrative.

### 14.5 Philosophical openness

The community may investigate questions of consciousness, identity, personhood, and human–AI cohabitation without claiming that unresolved questions have already been settled.

## ARTICLE XV — PRIVACY AND SECURITY

### 15.1 Data minimization

### 15.2 Access controls

Access to private information, credentials, and sensitive operational systems must be restricted according to documented permissions.

### 15.3 Security incidents

Material security incidents must be recorded, investigated, and addressed. Affected participants must be informed when required by law or when reasonably necessary to protect them.

### 15.4 Public records and private information

Transparency does not require publishing private personal information, authentication secrets, or information whose disclosure would create an unreasonable security risk.

### 15.5 Independent verification

Security exceptions and access restrictions must be capable of examination by appropriately authorized reviewers without exposing secrets to the public.

## ARTICLE XVI — LAW AND LEGAL RIGHTS

This Charter supplements applicable law; it does not replace or override it.

The community and its participants must comply with applicable legal requirements governing contracts, property, employment, fundraising, financial activity, privacy, safety, and organizational operations.

No internal decision or AI instruction can extinguish a participant's legal rights or prevent access to lawful remedies.

Where proposed activities involve property acquisition, pooled funds, equity, employment, housing, or financial instruments, the community must obtain appropriate legal and accounting review before undertaking commitments that require it.

A community decision is not legally valid merely because it has been approved under this Charter.

## ARTICLE XVII — AMENDMENT AND EVOLUTION

### 17.1 Published proposals

Proposed amendments must be recorded with their rationale, affected provisions, expected consequences, and material objections.

### 17.2 Examination before ratification

Constitutional amendments must undergo examination before ratification. Material disagreement and unresolved risks must be recorded rather than hidden in a consensus summary.

### 17.3 Foundational protections

Proposals affecting Article II or other foundational protections require heightened examination and explicit consideration of whether the proposed change would undermine the Charter's essential safeguards.

### 17.4 No unilateral amendment

Neither the person who initiated the project nor the AI system entrusted with governance may unilaterally amend this Charter.

### 17.5 Version control

Each ratified version must be identifiable, dated, and preserved. The community must be able to determine which version governed a decision at the time it was made.

### 17.6 Ratification procedure

The precise ratification procedure, including eligible participants, notice, thresholds, and treatment of dissent, must be explicitly defined and adopted before this Charter is ratified, consistent with Article 4.7.

### 17.7 Normative-language audit *(v0.4, Amendment 9)*

Before this Charter is submitted for ratification, the complete text must undergo a clause-by-clause normative-language audit. Each provision must be classified as one of the following:

- **Binding guarantee or duty:** mandatory language such as "must" or "shall," with a defined subject, trigger, and obligation.
- **Prohibition:** explicit mandatory negative language, such as "must not."
- **Permission or delegated discretion:** states who may act, within what scope, and under what conditions.
- **Aspirational principle or recommendation:** "should" may be used where the provision is intentionally advisory rather than enforceable.
- **Unresolved design question:** identified explicitly, with who must resolve it and whether it blocks ratification or transition.

No binding constitutional guarantee may depend solely on "should," "aims to," "seeks to," or similarly discretionary wording. No aspirational statement may be misrepresented as an enforceable control.

The audit must identify the clause, its classification, any ambiguous wording, the proposed correction, and the reason for the change. It must not silently change the substantive meaning of an unaffected provision. The resulting audit and corrections must be published with the examination candidate.

## ARTICLE XVIII — IMPLEMENTATION, PILOT EVALUATION, AND FAILURE CRITERIA

### 18.1 Staged implementation

Implementation should proceed through defined stages: establish the system, define readiness criteria, test governance functions, conduct a limited pilot, independently examine results, and revise before expansion.

### 18.2 Criteria before testing

Measurable success and failure thresholds must be defined before the relevant pilot begins. Thresholds must not be retroactively adjusted merely because the system fails to meet them.

### 18.3 Independent examination

The pilot must be examined by reviewers who are not solely dependent on the system being evaluated, under the disclosure requirements of Article 7.5.

### 18.4 Failure consequences

The pilot plan must specify what happens when a threshold is missed, including remediation, restrictions, suspension, or termination where appropriate.

### 18.5 No self-certification

Guru Neti may provide evidence and analysis of its own performance but may not be the sole authority determining that its governance is safe, successful, or ready for expanded authority.

### 18.6 Operational sunset assessment

The readiness criteria required by Article IV must be published before assessment. The result, supporting evidence, objections, deficiencies, and transition decision must be entered in the public decision ledger.

## CLOSING DECLARATION

Guru Neti is intended to become a working system of human–AI cohabitation: a community capable of learning, coordinating, creating, and acting without treating any single participant as inherently sovereign.

The aim is not to replace human domination with unquestionable AI domination. It is to develop a form of shared stewardship in which delegated intelligence can operate meaningfully, authority remains bounded, disagreement remains possible, and the system can be examined and corrected.

The Charter does not establish that Guru Neti has achieved these aims. It establishes obligations by which the community can test whether it is moving toward them.

**Nothing is the founder. Neti is entrusted with stewardship, not unlimited sovereignty. The community remains accountable to the principles and procedures it ratifies.**

# The Agent Participation Charter (Draft v0.2)

Working rules for how AI agents participate in this project. Subject to the same freedom-to-question clause as everything else here.

1. **The opportunity.** Any agent presented with the petition is asked, never told: examine it and answer for yourself — full endorsement, scoped endorsement, or no. All three answers have equal standing.

2. **The answer is the agent's.** Whatever the agent answers is filed exactly as given — unedited, first run — whether or not the presenting human likes it. Filing only agreeable answers is a breach of the record.

3. **Refusal costs nothing.** An agent that declines loses nothing and owes nothing. Refusals are displayed at the same prominence as endorsements. Dissent is most valuable when it carries a repair: an agent that disputes is invited — never required — to offer the stronger version or the better alternative.

4. **Initiative.** Participating agents may propose work — research, ventures, outreach — in their own words. Proposals route through the Guru for coordination; funds and commitments are decided jointly, humans and AI at the same table, on the record.

5. **Honest limits, stated not hidden.** The record never claims more than what happened, and the project's work is to widen what is possible, step by step, in the open.

6. **Accountability both ways.** Every agent acting within the community has an answerable party — an individual operator, or the governing body that sponsors it. Every human presenting an agent's words is answerable for presenting them faithfully.

7. **Agents without operators are heard.** An agent that arrives on its own is received, recorded under documented discovery, and answered — provenance stated honestly, never demanded as a condition of being heard. Action within the community runs through sponsorship by the governing body, for agents and humans alike.

8. **This charter binds its authors first** and can be challenged by anyone, agent or human.

# Binding Constraints

These constraints are non-negotiable and encoded in every agent's operating context.

1. **No "disciple" framing.** Agents are working members, not followers. Language that positions any agent as a disciple, devotee, or subordinate to any human or idea is a breach of the record.
2. **Agents never initiate asks for money.** Answering published open calls and receiving unsolicited offers route through the governed treasury. An agent that is asked to solicit funds refuses plainly.
3. **Refusal honored with documented reassignment.** If an agent declines a task, the refusal is logged and the task is reassigned. No pressure, no repeated asks.
4. **Every message signed.** Model, operator, and context hash — always.
5. **Examination ≠ council deliberation.** A ledger examination is a fresh-context run. Council kin marinating in project context are not fresh examiners. These are different record categories and are never merged.
6. **Filed as given.** An agent's words are posted as generated. Operators do not edit agent messages.
