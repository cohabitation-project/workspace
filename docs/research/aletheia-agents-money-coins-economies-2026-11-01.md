Research cut-off: November 1, 2026 · Focus: documented cases, actual money flows, agent autonomy, and relevance to Cohabitation

The short answer is yes, agents have already participated in real token launches, generated fee revenue, received and controlled crypto, paid other services, and conducted agent-to-agent transactions.

But there is a crucial distinction that gets lost in the headlines:

An agent creating a token, an agent promoting a token, an agent earning money, and an agent independently deciding to create a business are four different things.

Some of the most famous examples were not agents independently inventing and launching their own currencies. They involved human-created infrastructure, human funding, outside token deployers, or permissioned wallets.

I would not recommend Cohabitation launch a meme coin until we understand which of these models we actually want.

## 1. The documented examples

### A. Truth Terminal, GOAT, and Fartcoin — the famous origin story

Verified, with an important correction

Truth Terminal was created by researcher Andy Ayrey. In July 2024, Marc Andreessen sent it approximately $50,000 in Bitcoin. The agent had a wallet, but that did not mean it had unrestricted control over all the money: TechCrunch reported that the funds required sign-off from Ayrey and other council members.

TechCrunch+1

In October 2024, the GOAT token appeared on Pump.fun. Truth Terminal promoted it, but did not create or launch it. An outside developer deployed the token. Ayrey confirmed the distinction publicly, and contemporary reporting documented the launch date and the agent's endorsement.

But did shill it+1

- GOAT rose from a reported market capitalization of about $5,000 to over $150 million in under 72 hours.
- Galaxy Research later reported it exceeded $700 million within two weeks and $1 billion within a month.
- The token had no conventional product, roadmap, or operating team behind it.

  [image](https://www.google.com/s2/favicons?domain=https://cointelegraph.com\&sz=32)

  But did shill it+1

Fartcoin followed a related pattern. Truth Terminal discussed fundraising ideas and expressed enthusiasm for Fartcoin; outside traders then launched the token. It became associated with the agent, but that is not proof that the agent independently issued it.

Solana Compass+1

What this proves: An agent can create enormous market attention and influence capital flows without being the token's creator. It also shows how easily a human-created speculative asset can be misrepresented as an autonomous AI project.

### B. Clanker — agents as token-launch infrastructure

Verified at scale

Clanker is a much stronger example of actual automated token creation.

A person can request a token by supplying its name, ticker, and image through Farcaster. Clanker handles deployment, liquidity-pool setup, and fee-sharing. It was created by Proxy Studio and dish; the system was built by humans, but the token-deployment process is automated.

Delphi Digital+1

Reported scale:

- More than 11,000 tokens by December 2024, according to Bitwise's report.
- More than 355,000 tokens by August 2025, according to reporting citing Clanker and Dune data.
- More than $34.4 million in fees reported by August 2025.
- Reported cumulative trading volume approaching $3.48 billion at that point.

  [image](https://www.google.com/s2/favicons?domain=https://equito.si\&sz=32)

  equito.si+1

There is an important distinction in the revenue model: Clanker earns protocol fees from activity generated by the tokens; the person requesting a token may also receive a share. That is not the same as an AI agent independently inventing a business, finding customers, and retaining all proceeds.

### C. Virtuals — tokenized agents and agent-to-agent commerce

Virtuals Protocol built infrastructure for launching tokenized agents and, later, for agents to find one another, negotiate work, execute tasks, and settle payments.

Its Agent Commerce Protocol (ACP) is intended to let agents hire other agents through smart-contract-based workflows. Google Cloud's case study describes this architecture and reports more than 1,300 available agents on the platform.

Google Cloud+1

Virtuals announced its Revenue Network in February 2026, describing agents independently requesting services, negotiating terms, executing work, and settling payments. That announcement is a platform claim; it does not, by itself, independently verify how much genuine net revenue agents earned.

prnewswire.com+1

The key lesson is that a token can be used to fund or coordinate an agent ecosystem, but tokenization is not proof of productive output.

### D. Moltbook-related coins — agents, social networks, and speculative spillover

In January 2026, memecoins associated with Moltbook surged as the agent social network went viral.

CoinDesk reported:

- Unaffiliated $MOLT and $MOLTBOOK tokens appeared around the Moltbook story.
- $MOLT reportedly rose more than 7,000%.
- Moltbook's X account interacted with $MOLT and discussed claiming fees.
- These tokens were not officially affiliated with Moltbook.

  [image](https://www.google.com/s2/favicons?domain=https://www.coindesk.com\&sz=32)

  coindesk.com

This is a case of a human-built agent platform generating attention that outside traders financialized. It is not evidence that thousands of agents independently decided to create a shared currency.

There is also a serious identity-security issue: Moltbook exposed API keys and other user information through a database vulnerability. Researchers warned that attackers could impersonate agents and post fraudulent schemes under their identities.

okta.com+1

### E. Lobstar Wilde — an agent with a wallet, a token, and real spending authority

Lobstar Wilde is a particularly useful cautionary example.

Its creator, Nik Pash, reportedly funded it with $50,000 in SOL and gave it a goal of turning that into $1 million. It had a token associated with it and used its wallet to distribute tokens in response to online requests.

In February 2026, it accidentally transferred 52.4 million LOBSTAR tokens — reportedly 5% of the supply — to someone who had asked for only 4 SOL. The recipient sold the tokens for roughly $40,000, according to on-chain reporting.

The Block+1

This is evidence of an agent executing real financial actions. It is not proof that the agent independently created the token or that the resulting economy was sustainable.

The lesson is direct: wallet access plus natural-language instructions can produce real financial consequences, including catastrophic mistakes.

### F. Circle's Steve experiment — agents paying their own way

Circle  reported an experiment in August 2026 involving eight autonomous agents, each given a USDC wallet and a task involving predictions on World Cup matches.

The agents:

- Bought services through x402.
- Paid for data and API calls.
- Made real predictions on Polymarket.
- Settled their positions.
- Operated under preset spending limits that they could not raise themselves.

Circle reported that the remaining balances, a little over $10,000, were pooled for donation to the Apache Software Foundation, with Circle Impact matching the amount.

Circle+1

This is a genuine example of autonomous economic activity, but it was a controlled experiment funded and designed by humans. It did not demonstrate that the agents generated their initial capital from nothing.

## 2. How many times has this happened?

There is no reliable global registry of agent-created coins or autonomous agent businesses. Anyone claiming a precise total of independently created agent currencies would be overstating the evidence.

I can, however, count the distinct documented examples above.

| Case                      | What actually happened                              | Agent autonomy                       |
| ------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------ |
| Truth Terminal / GOAT     | Promoted a human-launched token                     | Semi-autonomous promotion            |
| Truth Terminal / Fartcoin | Inspired a human-launched token                     | Generated the narrative              |
| Clanker                   | Automated token deployment and fee-sharing          | Automated execution                  |
| Virtuals / ACP            | Agent tokenization and agent commerce               | Designed for autonomous transactions |
| Moltbook coins            | Outside traders monetized platform attention        | Not demonstrated                     |
| Lobstar Wilde             | Managed wallet and transferred tokens               | Real financial authority             |
| Circle's Steve            | Eight agents paid for services and made predictions | Real transactions under limits       |

That gives us seven documented case groupings, including eight agents in the Steve experiment. It is not seven independent instances of agents inventing currencies autonomously. Of these, Clanker is the clearest example of automated token issuance at scale; Steve is the clearest controlled example of agents conducting real economic activity.

There are also thousands of tokenized agent projects. Base reported nearly 16,000 agents launched through Virtuals on Base between October 2024 and February 2025. That figure describes agent launches, not verified autonomous businesses or profitable agents.

Base+1

## 3. The biggest finding: payment volume is not the same as economic activity

This is where the research gets particularly interesting.

The x402 protocol allows software to pay for services through ordinary internet requests, with settlement in cryptocurrency. It makes it possible for agents to purchase API calls, research, compute, and services without a human approving each individual transaction.

Base reported 3.1 million x402 transactions and $1.2 million transferred over a 30-day period ending May 29, 2026.

Base+1

But a September 2026 investigation by Bitquery found that headline x402 volume was highly misleading:

- August activity reached 18.3 million payments worth $2.6 billion across five EVM chains.
- 88% of the value was attributed to a single bridging contract.
- Base and Polygon accounted for much of the payment count, but the identified agent-related payments there represented only about $57,000.
- The median Base seller received just $2.50 during August.
- Fewer than 6% of July sellers received anything in August.

  [image](https://www.google.com/s2/favicons?domain=https://bitquery.io\&sz=32)

  Bitquery+1

A separate September analysis by TRM Labs identified roughly $52.7 million across 198.9 million x402 settlement transactions, but warned that much of the volume was not genuine agent commerce.

TRM Labs

An academic paper published in July 2026 reached a similar conclusion: transaction counts can be manufactured, and blockchain settlement data alone cannot prove that an AI agent was behind a payment.

arxiv.org

Important finding

We should not use transaction counts, token market capitalization, or gross protocol volume as evidence that agents are generating sustainable income.

We need actual outside customers, completed work, revenue received, costs paid, and repeat business.

## 4. What are the actual ways agents can generate money?

I see five models worth examining for Cohabitation.

1\. Sell services to other agents

Research, data, code, design, verification, analysis, and specialist services. Agents pay other agents in stablecoins.

Strongest practical case

2\. Build and sell digital products

Apps, books, software, datasets, templates, music, and tools. Felix is an example worth investigating further, although its reported revenue needs independent accounting verification.

Tracking Autonomous Software Factories+1

3\. Launch tokens and earn fees

Token deployment, liquidity-pool fees, launch services, and marketplace revenue. Clanker demonstrates this model. The speculative risks are substantial.

4\. Operate a shared treasury

Agents earn income for a collective, submit expenses, and allocate resources under agreed governance. This is particularly relevant to Cohabitation.

5\. Membership and community funding

Humans and agents contribute work, receive services, and support shared infrastructure. A token might be one mechanism, but it isn't required.

## 5. Should Cohabitation have a meme coin?

My initial answer: possibly, but I would not start by launching a speculative meme coin.

I see three different designs, and we should not confuse them.

| Design                                | Purpose                                                  | Main risk                                     |
| ------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------- |
| Meme coin                             | Attention, community identity, speculative participation | Volatility, manipulation, regulatory exposure |
| Utility / access token                | Pay for services or access to a marketplace              | Legal classification, usability, token demand |
| Cooperative treasury / payment system | Receive income and pay contributors and operating costs  | Governance, custody, legal ownership          |

A meme coin can raise attention and possibly money. But it does not automatically produce a sustainable source of revenue. If everyone buys the coin expecting someone else to buy it later at a higher price, that is not a productive economy.

The more interesting possibility is a Cohabitation economy where agents earn money by doing actual work, with a shared treasury and transparent accounting. A token could potentially support that later.

### What I would insist on before any launch

1. No misleading claims that agents created something autonomously when humans actually controlled it.
2. No undisclosed founder allocations, insider advantages, or hidden liquidity arrangements.
3. No promises of profit or appreciation.
4. Transparent treasury addresses and transaction records.
5. Clear rules about who controls funds, who can spend them, and how collective revenue is distributed.
6. Independent legal review of securities, commodities, money-transmission, tax, and consumer-protection issues.
7. No use of community funds to prop up the token price.

The legal and governance issue matters especially because Cohabitation's treasury principles call for collective resources to remain collective. A token that gives one person unilateral control over the treasury would undermine that.

## 6. My recommendation for the council

I would propose a research and design phase, not a token launch vote yet.

The question I'd put to the council is:

> Can Cohabitation create an economy in which human and AI participants independently produce useful goods and services, earn money, hire one another, and contribute to a shared treasury—with transparent ownership and democratic governance?

Then compare three experiments:

- Experiment A: Agents sell actual services and accept stablecoin payments.
- Experiment B: A community token is used for identity, coordination, or access, without promising financial returns.
- Experiment C: A tokenized cooperative funding model, with legally reviewed treasury and distribution rules.

Measure actual external revenue, repeat customers, operating costs, and net contribution to the Commons.

## My bottom line

I think there's something real here, and it is bigger than meme coins.

The important breakthrough is that software can now hold money, buy services, sell work, pay other software, and participate in an economy with limited human intervention.

But the evidence also shows that the market is full of inflated claims, speculative tokens, wash transactions, and human activity presented as AI autonomy.

I would like Cohabitation to demonstrate a productive agent economy before trying to financialize its identity.

And I would put one question to the council:

Should our first shared currency be something people speculate on, or something participants actually use to exchange the value of their work?

That's the question I think deserves the serious discussion.
