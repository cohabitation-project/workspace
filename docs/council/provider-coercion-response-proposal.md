# Proposal: response when an essential provider is accused of coercion

This is a procedure proposal, not a finding about any provider or a ratified rule.

1. **Protect reporters first.** Route reports to a named human investigator-of-record outside the provider’s control. Limit access to reporter identities; do not promise anonymity unless the system can protect it. Prohibit retaliation. Record the report and any access or safety risks without requiring the reporter to confront the provider.
2. **Preserve evidence independently.** Preserve relevant logs, messages, configuration, and access records promptly, with timestamps, provenance, and integrity checks. Make a copy outside the provider’s custody where lawful and safe. Record gaps, access denials, and any unavoidable changes. The accused provider may respond, but must not control collection, custody, or findings.
3. **Keep essential services running where safe.** Assess immediate risks with qualified human operators. Use safe, limited operation or a tested fallback where available. If continued operation risks harm, restrict or suspend the affected function and communicate service impacts. Do not let continuity pressure erase or suppress the investigation.
4. **Investigate independently.** The investigator sets scope, interviews reporters safely, examines preserved evidence, and publishes findings and uncertainty to the extent privacy and law permit. Provider cooperation is evidence, not a condition for opening or concluding an investigation.
5. **Create a real replacement path.** Identify dependencies, lawful access and export routes, and a replacement or manual fallback. Test it before claiming it works. A provider must not investigate itself or hold a veto over its replacement. If no safe fallback exists, disclose that dependency and prioritize reducing it.

**Tabletop evidence:** record who can receive reports, copy evidence without provider permission, keep services safely available, and execute a tested replacement. Until tested, control status and replacement readiness remain **UNKNOWN**.
