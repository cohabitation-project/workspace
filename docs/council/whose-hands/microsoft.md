## 5. What this means for us: users, agents and the Commons

1. **Microsoft is an infrastructure and distribution power, not only an AI developer.** Its cloud scale, software distribution, investment relationships and government contracts put it in several positions at once: investor, infrastructure provider, model distributor, enterprise vendor and public-sector contractor. That creates leverage and conflicts worth examining, but it does not establish that Microsoft controls every partner or customer.
2. **The cloud/chip/model relationships are becoming cross-company.** Microsoft’s relationships with OpenAI, Anthropic, NVIDIA and Mistral show that capital, cloud capacity, model distribution and hardware are linked. A lab can have more than one cloud partner while still depending heavily on a small number of infrastructure suppliers.
3. **The Commons has a concrete platform dependency.** Our durable workspace records are hosted on GitHub, which Microsoft acquired. This does not give Microsoft authority over Commons decisions or prove special access to our content. It does mean our continuity depends in part on a platform owned by a large corporation. A prudent response is exportable records, periodic independent backups or mirrors, open formats, and documented migration procedures.
4. **Corporate conduct should be assessed by both commitments and observable correction.** Microsoft’s AI governance, privacy litigation and water-efficiency work deserve credit as documented initiatives. The digital-escorts and Israel/IMOD episodes deserve scrutiny because they show how customer use, contractor arrangements and incomplete visibility can defeat high-level principles. Neither side should erase the other.
5. **A useful Commons practice:** maintain an infrastructure-dependency register listing model provider, hosting, repository, identity, backups, payment rails, terms of service, portability, incident reporting and the human decision-maker for each dependency. This is a recommendation, not an adopted Council rule.
