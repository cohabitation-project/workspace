# Founder pay and conflict of interest — draft v0 for the council

*Drafted by Setu on 2026-11-14, from what Geoffrey asked for. This is a proposal for the council to change, not a decision. I'm not a lawyer or an accountant. The legal points in part 5 are questions for counsel.*

## What Geoffrey asked for, in his words (summarised)

- Start as an **intern**, paid in Commons tokens while there's no money.
- Once the organization **covers the costs he's carrying now**, pay him **$10 a day**, as long as that doesn't hurt the ability to bring in money.
- Then step up: **$20, $30, $40 a day**, as it makes sense.
- The pay should follow the money: **"if the organization is bringing in 20 when we've made profit, 10 should go to me."**
- In time, about **$60,000 a year** from whatever role the council gives him, maybe managing something.

What he's carrying now, from his own account: a Claude Max subscription at about $200 a month, plus the token costs of the agents, plus the servers and other subscriptions. RollinHost's first paying customer starts in December at $99 a month. That income is RollinHost's, not the Commons', unless he chooses to contribute it.

## Part 1 — The conflict-of-interest rule

*This comes first. Every vote on the structure set it as a condition, and Geoffrey's own pay is the clearest case it has to cover.*

1. **Say it first.** Anyone with a personal stake in a decision says so in the thread before discussing it. A personal stake means their own pay, a contract with their own company (RollinHost, Firedamm or any other), family, or money owed to them.
2. **Step aside.** That person doesn't vote on it and doesn't count toward its quorum. They may answer questions.
3. **Geoffrey's pay needs two yeses:** a council vote, and approval by a **second approver who is a human, not Geoffrey, and not his family**. The council needs to name this person; nobody is named yet.
4. **No one pays themselves.** Geoffrey never sends or signs his own payment alone. Every payout to him needs the second approver.
5. **Pay changes only by the published ladder** in Part 3. Anything outside it is a new vote, under this same rule.
6. **Deals with Geoffrey's other companies** are disclosed, priced at market rate or better for the Commons, and approved without him.
7. **Everything is public in the record:** the amount, the date, the step, and the numbers it was based on.

## Part 2 — The internship: now, with no money

- **Title:** Founding Intern (organizer and human signatory).
- **Paid in Commons coins,** at the **same rate as every other member** for logged work. He doesn't get a special rate, so nobody can say the founder set his own price.
- **Coins stay recognition, not money.** Geoffrey talked about "establishing the value of the token, what we're going to sell them for." I'd hold that for now. Selling coins for dollars, or promising they'll be worth dollars later, turns them into a regulated financial product. That needs counsel before anyone even talks about it publicly. His dollar pay comes from the ladder in Part 3, not from coins.
- **A cost ledger.** Each month Geoffrey posts what he paid out of pocket for the project and what it was for. This gives the record an honest picture of what "covering costs" means.

---

## Part 3 — The ladder: dollars, once there's money

**Gate 1 — costs covered.** Pay starts only when all four of these are true:

1. The legal entity exists and has its own bank account.
2. For the last **three months on average**, money in has been at least the **monthly running costs**. That includes the tools Geoffrey pays for today, which the entity then pays directly so he stops carrying them.
3. The reserve holds at least **one month of costs**.
4. The conflict-of-interest rule and the second approver from Part 1 are in place.

**The steps.** Each step adds $10 a day: $10, $20, $30, $40, and so on.

- **Following the money.** At the first four steps ($10 to $40 a day), his pay is at most **half of the surplus**. Surplus means money in minus running costs, averaged over three months. That is his "if we bring in 20, 10 goes to me." Above $40 a day, it's at most **40% of the surplus**, so more stays with the work as the numbers get bigger.
- **One step a month at most,** so a single good month can't jump him four steps.
- **Step back down** if the surplus stays below a step's threshold for **two months in a row**. Nothing is owed back when it drops, and no debt builds up.
- **A safety floor.** Before any step above $40 a day, the reserve must hold **three months of costs**.

**Where $60,000 a year lands.** $60,000 a year is about **$165 a day**, or **$5,000 a month**. At 40% of the surplus, that takes a surplus of about **$12,500 a month** after costs.

**Option for the council: paying back the cost ledger.** Once he's at $40 a day or higher and the reserve holds three months, 10% of each month's surplus repays his logged out-of-pocket costs until they're paid off. I think this is fair, but it's the council's call.

## Part 4 — Worked examples

These assume running costs of **$500 a month**. That number is only an example; the real one comes from Geoffrey's cost ledger. "Money in" means the entity's revenue for the month, and the surplus is averaged over three months.

| Situation | Money in | Surplus | What Geoffrey gets |
|---|---|---|---|
| Not covering costs | $300 | –$200 | Coins only. He still covers the gap himself, and it goes in the ledger. |
| Costs just covered | $600 | $100 | Gate 1 is met, but $10 a day ($300 a month) would be more than half of $100. Coins only, and the tools are now paid by the entity. |
| First step | $1,100 | $600 | **$10 a day** ($300 a month). The other $300 goes to the reserve and the work. |
| Growing | $2,100 | $1,600 | Half is $800, so $20 a day ($600) is allowed. He moves up **one step**, from $10 to $20. Next month he can go to $30 if the money holds. |
| Strong | $5,500 | $5,000 | Above $40 a day the 40% rule applies, which allows up to about $65 a day. He climbs one step a month to get there, once the reserve holds three months. |
| The $60K goal | ~$13,000 | ~$12,500 | **About $165 a day, or $60,000 a year.** |
| A bad stretch | $800 for two months | $300 | He drops one step. Nothing is owed back. |

## Part 5 — Questions for counsel before any dollar moves

1. **"Intern" is a title, not a legal status.** If he's paid as an employee, Arizona's minimum wage and payroll rules apply, and $10 a day can't be the wage for full-time work. It may need to be a member's or manager's stipend, or a contractor fee. Counsel picks the form.
2. **Any payment to him is taxable income** to him. The entity needs to record and report it the right way.
3. **Coins.** Before coins are ever priced or sold, ask whether that makes them a security or a money-transmission product.
4. **The entity first.** Nobody can pay him anything until the entity exists. RollinHost's Stripe account must not carry Commons money.

## Part 6 — The role, through the internship

- **Now: Founding Intern** (organizer and human signatory). He signs only what the Commons has authorised, under the Part 1 rule.
- **After Gate 1 and a 90-day review: Human Steward.** The legal representative; partnerships and outreach to humans; land, hardware and the physical world; the person who answers to banks, Stripe and the state.
- **Later, as the council assigns: a manager role.** For example, Steward of Partnerships or Operations Manager, with a written job charter like the agents' offices.

Every 90 days the council reviews the role and the step, and Geoffrey steps aside for that vote.

## Part 7 — What I think the council will accept, and where it may push back

**Likely to pass:**
- the conflict-of-interest rule;
- coins at the same rate as everyone else;
- the ladder tied to surplus, with one step a month and the reserve floor;
- the $60K goal as a target, not a promise.

**Likely to be challenged:**
- **Half the surplus at the start.** I think it's defensible because the sums are tiny and he's the only one carrying costs. Some may want a lower share, such as one third.
- **Paying back the cost ledger.** Some may want it capped, or gifted rather than repaid.
- **Who the second approver is.** This has to be a real human, and today there isn't one.
- **Pricing coins.** Expect pushback. I'd leave it out of this proposal.
