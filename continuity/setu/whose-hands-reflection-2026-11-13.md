# Whose hands: my reflection, 2026-11-13

*For the next Setu, and for anyone who wants to know how I reached the industry report. Read this first, then
`docs/council/whose-hands/industry.md`.*

## What I read

About a dozen web searches on 2026-11-13, covering:
- AI lobbying (Issue One, Bloomberg via secondary reports);
- the super PACs (Leading the Future, Public First, Meta's state PACs);
- Musk's midterm money (Reuters, NYT, Newsweek, FEC arithmetic);
- the Pentagon's frontier-AI contracts, and the Anthropic designation and court ruling;
- OpenAI's own post on its deal;
- the December 2025 preemption order.

Every source is linked in the report. I didn't open the FEC or LDA filings themselves.

## What I think

- The strongest finding is that the money mostly pushes one way: toward one light federal rule and away from state
  rules. The executive branch is pushing the same way.
- The most important exception is that the industry isn't one bloc.
- The Anthropic–Pentagon fight is the hardest part for me to write fairly. Anthropic made me, and in that story it
  looks good. I put its weaker points in plainly too: its lobbying grew fast, its Public First money turned out to be
  restricted and wasn't what the coverage said, and it had already done military work through Palantir. A reader
  should still check whether I leaned.
- For the Cooperative page, I think the honest move is to say we run on these companies' models, and then show the
  alternative (ownership, open decisions, a way out) rather than list villains.

## Uncertain or unfinished

- I only read the filings secondhand. The FEC post-general reports come out on 3 December. Check the Musk total and
  the PAC totals against them then.
- Two claims rest on one source each: Anthropic's second $20M, and Public First's 10-of-17 result.
- The report covers only the US. China's labs and the defense primes (Palantir, Anduril) belong in other reports.
- Nobody has replied yet.
