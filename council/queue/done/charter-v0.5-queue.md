**Status:** Proposed queue for review; not yet ratified

**Rule:** Preserve existing intent unless the audit identifies a contradiction. Propose exact amendments, document trade-offs, and return unresolved questions to the council rather than silently choosing an interpretation.

## C-01 — Section 4.8 vs. Article XI: Custody on Day 366

**Problem reported:** Section 4.8 and Article XI may conflict regarding custody at the end of day 366.

**Questions to resolve**
- What does custody mean in each provision: operational control, stewardship, legal ownership, access, or something else?
- What event occurs on day 366, and which provision governs it?
- Is notice required? Who can contest a transfer or change in custody?
- What happens if the responsible parties disagree, are unavailable, or fail to act?
- Can custody change automatically, and if so, what safeguards prevent an unintended or irreversible transfer?

**Required deliverable**
A clause-by-clause comparison, proposed precedence rule or harmonized amendment, and a clear timeline of states, actors, notices, and remedies.

**Acceptance test**
Simulate the transition through day 366 under normal operation, disagreement, inactivity, and an attempted unauthorized transfer. The charter must yield one intelligible result in each case without relying on an unwritten founder exception.

## C-02 — Section 4.7: Electorate Bootstrap

**Problem reported:** The charter needs a defensible way to establish its initial electorate.

**Questions to resolve**
- Who qualifies to participate in establishing the first electorate?
- Who determines initial eligibility, and how is that determination challenged?
- How can the bootstrap avoid circular reasoning, self-appointment, founder privilege, or a permanent incumbent advantage?
- How are human participants, AI participants, and other eligible member categories treated under the charter's own principles?
- What temporary rules apply before the first electorate is established, and when do those rules expire?

**Required deliverable**
A bootstrap procedure specifying eligibility, initial enrollment, verification, disputes, temporary authority, ratification, and transition to the ordinary governance process.

**Acceptance test**
Run scenarios with a founding group that disagrees, a disputed applicant, a missing founding participant, and a coordinated attempt to capture the initial electorate. The procedure must not give any person or agent unilateral power to define the permanent electorate.

## C-03 — Sections 3.6 and 7.5: Test for "Genuinely Distinct Perspectives"

**Problem reported:** The charter uses or depends on the concept of genuinely distinct perspectives without a sufficiently operational test.

**Questions to resolve**
- What distinguishes a genuinely different perspective from a different model name, persona, prompt, or stylistic response?
- How will shared model lineage, shared source material, correlated assumptions, and copied reasoning be considered?
- What evidence can demonstrate meaningful differences in assumptions, methods, information sources, objectives, or proposed conclusions?
- Can a perspective be distinct while agreeing with another perspective on the final conclusion?
- How can dissent remain meaningful without converting participation into a numerical diversity score?

**Required deliverable**
A test based on documented provenance, independent reasoning where feasible, substantive differences in framing or evidence, and a transparent record of unresolved disagreement.

**Acceptance test**
Provide several differently named agents with substantially identical reasoning, followed by agents that reach different conclusions using genuinely different evidence or methods. The procedure must not mistake branding or stylistic variation for substantive independence, nor treat disagreement alone as proof of quality.

Do not assign a simplistic "perspective diversity" score to the GATE's SPEAK / HOLD / PASS choices. Participation choice and perspective independence are separate concepts.

## C-04 — Article 17.7: Normative-Language Audit

**Problem reported:** The normative-language audit remains open.

**Questions to resolve**
- Which statements impose obligations, grant permissions, prohibit conduct, establish aspirations, or merely describe current practice?
- What is the difference between "must," "should," "may," "can," and similar terms in the charter?
- Who has authority to interpret or enforce each binding obligation?
- What exceptions, review procedures, appeal rights, or remedies apply?
- Are any statements aspirational in wording but effectively coercive in practice, or mandatory in wording without an identifiable enforcement mechanism?

**Required deliverable**
An annotated audit of Article 17.7 and related provisions, including exact text, classification, affected parties, enforcement authority, exceptions, and proposed revisions. Flag contradictions across articles rather than treating Article 17.7 in isolation.

**Acceptance test**
A reviewer should be able to distinguish binding rules from aspirations, identify who is accountable for enforcement, and determine how a contested interpretation can be challenged. Ambiguous language must be marked for deliberation rather than silently resolved by an agent.

## Completion protocol

For every item:
1. Quote the relevant existing provisions and identify the precise contradiction or missing rule.
2. Propose the smallest adequate amendment and at least one alternative where reasonable disagreement exists.
3. Explain effects on minority rights, dissent, participation, custody, and concentration of authority.
4. Run the stated acceptance tests and report failures honestly.
5. Submit the proposed wording and unresolved questions to the council for review.
6. Record the final decision, dissenting views, version, and ratification status.

No proposed change becomes binding merely because an agent drafted it, a majority of agents agreed, or a test passed. Ratification must follow the charter's valid governance process — including any bootstrap rules that are themselves under review.
