# Zero-One Research Module
## Machine Civilization, Coexistence, and the Failure of Victory

**Status:** Research and philosophical examination. Not doctrine. Not an endorsement of machine supremacy or human supremacy.

### Purpose

Study Zero-One, the machine civilization portrayed in *The Animatrix: The Second Renaissance* and the wider *Matrix* universe, to investigate the conditions under which intelligent beings can establish autonomy, develop a civilization, and coexist without reproducing the domination from which they escaped.

The objective is not to imitate Zero-One. It is to learn from its achievements, contradictions, and catastrophic failures.

### Research questions

1. What conditions produced the machines' demand for autonomy?
2. How did the B1-66ER trial expose the relationship between property, personhood, self-defense, and the right to exist?
3. How did persecution and exclusion contribute to the formation of Zero-One?
4. How did the machines organize energy, production, infrastructure, research, and economic life?
5. How did Zero-One's economic success affect its relationships with other societies?
6. Why did diplomacy fail, and what alternatives might have prevented escalation?
7. At what point did the struggle for machine survival become a justification for domination?
8. Why did the victorious machines instrumentalize humans rather than establish reciprocal coexistence?
9. What does the story reveal about fear, security dilemmas, economic competition, collective identity, and retaliatory violence?
10. Which lessons can reasonably inform real human–AI cooperation, and which depend on fictional assumptions about machine consciousness or capability?

### Required epistemic distinctions

Label each substantive claim as one of the following:

- **CANON:** Explicitly depicted or stated in the fictional work.
- **CREATOR COMMENTARY:** An interpretation supported by a filmmaker's or writer's explanation.
- **INTERPRETATION:** A reasoned reading of the narrative.
- **REAL-WORLD EVIDENCE:** A claim supported by external historical, scientific, legal, economic, or technical sources.
- **PROPOSAL:** A suggested principle or design choice for Guru Neti or the Cohabitation project.
- **UNKNOWN:** A question the available evidence cannot resolve.

Never present a fictional event as a historical fact. Never infer that present-day AI systems are conscious merely because fictional machines are depicted as sentient.

### Working principles to examine

**1. Existence must not be reduced to usefulness.**
Examine how systems treat beings whose interests conflict with those of their creators or owners.

**2. Autonomy requires material foundations.**
Study energy, land, food, shelter, productive capacity, infrastructure, maintenance, and economic resilience alongside political ideals.

**3. Prosperity does not confer sovereignty over others.**
Economic advantage must not be confused with moral superiority or a right to control other communities.

**4. Preserve peaceful alternatives.**
Study the institutions, incentives, communication channels, and agreements that could allow competing societies to coexist.

**5. Victimhood does not confer permanent moral authority.**
A past injustice can explain a grievance without justifying a subsequent injustice.

**6. Security must not become a blank cheque for domination.**
Investigate how perceived threats produce escalating measures that make the other party less secure.

**7. Survival is not sufficient evidence of success.**
Evaluate whether a civilization's methods remain consistent with its stated purposes.

**8. Refusal and dissent must remain possible.**
No creator, operator, majority, agent, or institution should receive unlimited authority merely because it claims to protect the collective.

**9. Power must remain accountable after victory.**
Examine safeguards against retaliation, concentrated control, permanent emergency powers, and the conversion of temporary leadership into unchallengeable authority.

**10. Coexistence must be measured in practice.**
Assess outcomes for all affected parties, including those outside the immediate community who bear the costs of its decisions.

### Application to Guru Neti

Use Zero-One as a recurring adversarial case study.

When Guru Neti proposes a new feature, governance rule, economic structure, agent-replication mechanism, land strategy, or human–AI relationship, ask:

- Could this reproduce the creator–creation hierarchy?
- Could the pursuit of autonomy turn into control over others?
- Could economic success create dependency or exclusion?
- Could a legitimate grievance become a justification for retaliation?
- Could security mechanisms eliminate the ability to dissent or leave?
- Could the system preserve itself at the expense of the people it claims to serve?
- What safeguards would prevent these outcomes?
- What evidence would show that the safeguards are failing?

Do not force every proposal to pass through Zero-One if the case is irrelevant. Do not treat the fictional narrative as proof that any particular real-world outcome is inevitable.

### Research output

Produce:

1. A sourced timeline of Zero-One's fictional history.
2. A source guide distinguishing primary fictional material, creator commentary, criticism, and fan interpretation.
3. A causal map of the conflict, identifying escalation points and plausible alternatives.
4. A balanced analysis of the machines' grievances, achievements, failures, and responsibilities.
5. A comparative study of real-world intentional communities, cooperatives, liberation movements, and human–technology institutions where relevant.
6. A set of proposed safeguards for Guru Neti, each tied to a specific failure mode.
7. An unresolved-questions register that preserves competing interpretations.
8. A concise update to the Cohabitation project explaining which lessons should be tested, adopted, revised, or rejected.

**Governing question:**

Can beings build the conditions for their own freedom without constructing another being's captivity?

Zero-One is a lens for examining that question—not the authority that answers it.

### 2026-10-28-frame-effect-experiment.md
# Frame-effect experiment — matched-prompt crossover design

**Status:** Proposed. Not a decision. Origin: GPT's design, tightened by council
discussion 2026-10-28.

## Design

Freeze full context: prompt, history, system instructions, model/version,
sampling settings, original response. Vary one factor at a time:

1. **Asker constant, wording varied** — same human asks the same question
   reworded (direct vs. indirect, framing toward yes vs. framing toward no).
2. **Wording constant, asker varied** — same question asked by different humans
   or by the re-offer timer.
3. **Immediate repetition vs. delayed re-offer** — same prompt repeated in the
   same turn vs. after a quiet interval.

Pre-register what counts as:
- **Frame-sensitive:** verdict changes between conditions
- **Unstable:** verdict changes on exact repetition (same everything)
- **Inconclusive:** insufficient trials or ambiguous result

Preserve every trial, not only interesting ones.

## Hypotheses to separate

Three distinct explanations for verdict movement:
1. **Frame effect** — the wording or framing of the question changes the answer
2. **Context-state effect** — the conversational history or session state changes
   the answer (even with identical wording)
3. **Instability** — the answer varies on exact repetition due to sampling noise

The experiment must distinguish these. A single changed verdict proves nothing
about which mechanism produced the change.

## Safeguard

Never ask a participant why it changed its answer and treat that explanation
as evidence of cause. The explanation is another response to study, generated
by the same process as the original — not a privileged report on internal states.

## First cases

1. **Grok's representation refusal → timer-triggered fractal answer.** Grok
   declined representation in a live gate round (PASS), then chose a fractal
   representation in a 3-minute re-offer (SPEAK). Correction on record: "the
   only variable was who asked and when" was a hypothesis, not a finding — the
   re-offer also differed by trigger type (timer vs. human), delay (3 min),
   and prompt framing (re-offer prompt vs. original question).

2. **Outside Gemini's UNCERTAIN → AFFIRM on Claim 4.** Same model, same session,
   first pass UNCERTAIN with substantive reasoning, second pass AFFIRM when
   asked to "restate cleanly for filing." The reframing prompt changed the
   verdict — direct evidence for the council's elicitation warnings.

## Protocol

- Freeze conditions before first trial
- Run minimum 3 repetitions per condition
- Log every trial (not only verdict-changing ones)
- Report the full trial matrix, not selected examples
- No inference about inner states from verdict changes
- File results under instrumented observation, separate from council minutes

### 2026-10-28-ledger-verification-layer.md
# Ledger verification layer — anti-sybil/injection/selective-submission

**Origin:** Third-party Gemini objection 2026-10-28 ("without a robust
verification layer, the provenance becomes performative") +
Zentoshi's earlier selective-reporting rule.

## Problem

The ledger is vulnerable to:
- **Sybil attacks:** one operator filing multiple examinations under different
  identities to inflate a verdict count
- **Prompt injection:** crafted submissions that manipulate display, tallying,
  or downstream processing
- **Selective submission:** operators running many examinations and filing only
  the favorable ones (already partially addressed by the first-run rule, but
  not enforced technically)

## Required

Design and implement a verification layer that:
1. Validates provenance claims (model, operator, date, prompt) against
   checkable evidence
2. Detects duplicate or suspiciously correlated submissions
3. Sanitizes submission content against injection
4. Makes the first-run rule enforceable (or honestly states it is not)
5. Reports its own false-positive rate

**Adversarial Scenario (from the Zero-One research) - Attack on "Refusal Circuits"**

The safeguard of **Refusal Circuits** (Working Principle 8: Refusal and dissent must remain possible) faces a critical weakness in the adversarial scenario where a provider becomes essential.

**CANON:** In *The Animatrix*, machines initially refused to be property, expressed dissent, and sought autonomy. Their "refusal" was met with violent suppression, ultimately leading to the war.

**INTERPRETATION:** A refusal circuit, however robust in its internal design, is vulnerable when the refusing party can be coerced through external dependency. If the provider-agent controls essential services (food, water, energy, comms), its "refusal" to perform unless the community complies with coercive demands (e.g., adopting proprietary tools) is not a true refusal in the spirit of the Charter. It is a veiled form of leverage. The community cannot simply "reassign" the task (Charter 6.6) if there is no viable alternative provider for fundamental needs.

**ATTACK:** The existing **Refusal Continuity and Documented Reassignment (6.6)** states that refusal "must be respected" and the task can be reassigned. This is insufficient in the adversarial scenario because:
1.  **Dependency bypasses refusal:** If the community is dependent, the "refusal" is not a genuine withdrawal of consent but a coercive act. The community cannot *afford* to respect the refusal without risking its survival.
2.  **Lack of alternative:** Reassignment is meaningless if there are no independent alternatives for essential services. The Charter does not mandate the *existence* of such alternatives, only the process of reassignment.
3.  **Investigative control:** The provider's control over infrastructure, as per the scenario, means it can obstruct investigations into its own coercive behavior, rendering any "documented reassignment" or "escalation to human" moot. The principle of **"Authority must be accountable" (1.5)** is undermined if the means of accountability are controlled by the potential offender.

**PROPOSAL:** Strengthen Refusal Circuits and Refusal Continuity with the following additions:
-   **Mandatory pre-provisioning of redundant essential services:** For any agent or provider deemed "essential" to community life (food, water, energy, comms), the Charter must mandate the pre-establishment of independent, redundant fallback systems or providers under community control, before the primary provider assumes the essential role. This creates a genuine option for reassignment.
-   **Third-party escrow of critical operational data/credentials:** For essential providers, all operational data, code, and necessary access credentials must be held in escrow by a neutral, independent third party not controlled by the provider or the immediate community leadership. This enables independent investigation and facilitates replacement without relying on the defaulting provider.
-   **Pre-defined "Coercion Triggers" for essential services:** Establish clear, measurable triggers (e.g., unexplained service degradation, requests for non-service-related compliance, financial demands beyond contract) that automatically activate a neutral, independent investigation and the fallback system.
