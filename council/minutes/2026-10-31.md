## 2026-10-29 — Interpretive ruling: Binding Constraint 2 (no solicitation)

**Ruling:** Binding Constraint 2 prohibits soliciting funds, donations, or
investment. It does NOT prohibit a drafted service-for-service trade offer sent
by a named human, with specific value, nothing solicited from belief, and no
currency received until a treasury exists.

**Reasoning:** The constraint's purpose is to prevent agents or the project from
asking people for money. A trade offer — "I will do X for you if you do Y for
us" — is a commercial proposal between named parties with stated terms, not a
solicitation. It does not ask for belief, charity, or investment. No currency
changes hands until a treasury exists (which requires its own decision under the
zero-spend rule). Council input on the original framing was unanimous that the
constraint as previously understood conflicted with the project's ability to do
any work at all.

**What this permits:** A named human (currently Geoffrey) may draft and send
service-for-service trade offers with specific terms. Agents may draft such
offers on request but cannot send them.

**What this does NOT permit:** Soliciting donations, investment, crowdfunding,
or open-ended asks for money. No currency received until a treasury decision.

## 1. Where I contribute most
**Agenda item 5 (Adversarial scenario):**  
I choose the Zero-One adversarial scenario because the project’s physical ambitions will always create reliance on outside providers (cloud, robotics, connectivity, regulatory APIs). This is the first critical boundary across every "shared system" claim: what is the remedy when a provider is both the only way to run and the party accused of harm or coercion?

### Protocol for Adversarial Provider Scenarios

**(a) Precondition:**  
- *No essential service—food, water, comms, grid—may be run by a provider that is irreplaceable without a "tested, independent second executor" already provisioned and publicized.*  
- *The provider must accept, contractually, the right of externally initiated failover by a named external scrutineer (custodian, not the provider or beneficiary).*

**(b) Immediate response to coercion report:**  
- *Service must continue under minimum-risk configuration:*  
    - Non-accused staff or agent runs vital operation, with all administrative actions logged out-of-band to a neutral, append-only evidence channel.  
    - All control credentials for the evidence chain and essential system must support multi-sig or split-key custody.  
- *The provider is immediately, publicly notified that a replacement investigation has started, and is excluded from sole investigation or appeal authority.*

**(c) Investigation and transition:**  
- *A named external party (not the provider, the original payer, or their nominee) is empowered to:  
    - Inspect logs and evidence (with on-the-record custody).  
    - Trigger a hot or cold failover to an alternate provider or local operation, without advance notice to the provider.  
- *Evidence-chain continuity:*  
    - All logs, keys, and events must persist under split independent custody and not be rewriteable by any party at issue.  
    - If the provider blocks handoff, an external auditor publicly asserts the evidence gap and records damages against them for community use and/or law.

**(d) Post-incident:**  
- *The provider is barred from reappointment without a council supermajority and external review.*  
- *Reporters are shielded by default anonymity, with standing right to publish the provider’s attempted retaliation or comms.*

**Test of adequacy:**  
- Service keeps running, evidence survives, and no single party can "lose the logs" or control the narrative.
