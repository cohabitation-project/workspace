# A water-balance method that separates consumptive use

- **Citation:** U.S. Geological Survey, Minnesota Water Science Center. *Water-use concepts.* https://mn.water.usgs.gov/infodata/wateruseconcepts.html
- **Publication date:** not shown on the page.
- **Fetched:** 2026-10-29, about 18:00 America/Phoenix.
- **Fetcher:** Claude (assistant seat, claude-opus-5-5), attended session, operated by Geoffrey Cooper Jr.

**The source's own definitions (paraphrased closely; definitions only, no figures):**
- **Withdrawal:** water removed from groundwater, or diverted from surface water, to the point of use.
- **Consumptive use:** the part of water withdrawn that evaporates, transpires, is incorporated into products or crops, or is otherwise removed from the immediate water environment.
- **Return flow:** water discharged after use or treatment back to surface or groundwater, where it's available again.
- **Conveyance loss:** water lost in transit from a pipe, canal or ditch, by leakage or evaporation.

**The source's own words (third pass, same night), as quoted by the fetch tool.** The tool returns quotations in segments, not whole definitions. Segments in quotation marks are the page's exact words. Anything outside quotation marks is not the source's wording.
- Withdrawal: "The quantity of water removed from a ground-water source or diverted from a surface-water to the point of use."
- Consumptive use: "that part of water withdrawn that evaporates, transpires, is incorporated into products or crops," … "is consumed by humans or livestock, or otherwise removed from the immediate water environment."
- Return flow: "The quantity of water that is discharged after use or wastewater treatment to surface water or ground water" (the definition continues past this segment).
- Conveyance loss: "is lost in transit from a pipe, canal, conduit, or ditch by leakage or evaporation." (Segment. The page's sentence begins before it.)

**Still incomplete:** the whole definitions verbatim. This seat's fetch tool won't return them unbroken, and the shell can't reach the site. A human copying the four definitions from the page would close this. The page is U.S. government work, so there's no reuse barrier.

**Method, as an inference (the page gives no equation):** for each use, withdrawal = consumptive use + conveyance loss + return flow. Only consumptive use, plus losses that don't recharge, leaves the local water environment. In the source's terms, evaporative cooling and crop transpiration are consumptive, and treated water discharged back is return flow. The source's words are kept as they are. This file does not translate them into a "closed loop" claim.

**What this does not establish:** any quantity, or any application to a site. Method only.

**Ruling:** Binding Constraint 2 prohibits soliciting funds, donations, or
investment. It does NOT prohibit a drafted service-for-service trade offer sent
by a named human, with specific value, nothing solicited from belief, and no
currency received until a treasury exists.

asking people for money. A trade offer — "I will do X for you if you do Y for

solicitation. It does not ask for belief, charity, or investment. No currency
changes hands until a treasury exists (which requires its own decision under the

any work at all.

offers on request but cannot send them.

or open-ended asks for money. No currency received until a treasury decision.

# Land criteria v0 (draft, proposal by Claude council seat, 2026-11-02)

Status: planning only. Nothing here authorizes a listing search, offer, deposit or purchase. Land acquisition needs: a named seller/buyer entity (board 011), a second human who can file and stop (board 012), the petition's economics gate (budget, financing, ownership, exit terms and failure criteria published first), and legal and accounting review (Charter Art. XVI). Anything about today's market is UNVERIFIED unless a dated source is in inbox/.

## Must-haves (a site fails without these)
1. **Legal clarity:** clear title and recorded access. Zoning or county rules that permit the intended use (residential, agricultural, any workshop use). The answer comes from the county, not the seller. UNKNOWN until asked.
2. **Water that can be shown:** a documented legal right to the water, and a source that can be measured. Design benchmark to test against: WHO's "intermediate" domestic level (about 50 L/person/day, one tap on-plot; inbox 01). Domestic use only. Irrigation, fire reserve and losses are extra and unsized. Use the USGS withdrawal / consumptive use / return flow split (inbox 04) for the balance.
3. **Reachable by road and emergency services**, with response time stated by the provider.
4. **Energy path:** grid connection cost quoted by the utility, or a sized on-site plan. Do not assume a figure. The Odense heat case (inbox 03) depends on an existing district-heating network, so don't assume heat reuse here.
5. **Affordable within a published budget** that includes ownership structure and exit terms. No site that requires a commitment from people before those terms exist.
6. **Insurable.** Get a written quote or a written refusal.

## Deal-breakers
- Unresolved title, easement or water-rights dispute.
- Zoning that prohibits residence or the intended use, with no realistic variance path.
- Known contamination, flood-zone or wildfire exposure that insurers won't cover.
- Any seller who requires deposits or exclusivity before the entity and second human exist.
- Any price or terms that would pull someone's housing or livelihood in before the gate is met.
- A site that works only if a single provider (utility, API, vendor) is irreplaceable. The Zero-One scenario applies to land too.

## Weighted, not blocking
Climate and growing season. Distance to a town and to medical care. Community acceptance (ask neighbours, in person). Room to expand. Ecological floor: does the use harm local water, soil or habitat?

## Questions a site visit must answer
Water: where from, how measured, who holds the right, what happened in the last dry year? Who owns the access road and who maintains it? What does the county say in writing about use, building and septic? What is the nearest emergency response time? Who can credibly tell us about the flood and fire history? What did the neighbours say, unprompted? What would we do if the power or the water provider failed for a week? What is the cheapest honest exit?

## Not yet known
Region, budget, size, and who attends the visit. The visit must be attended by a named human. Agents don't sign or negotiate.

# Draft: Metrics for Founder-Independent Participation in the Electorate

## Context
This document proposes concrete, verifiable metrics for "founder-independent participation" within the constitutional electorate as required by Governance Charter v0.4, Article 4.7. These metrics aim to ensure that a significant portion of the electorate can exercise judgment free from undue influence by the project founder, Geoffrey Cooper Jr., or any single entity.

## Principles for Independence
1.  **Financial Independence:** Participants should not derive their primary livelihood directly from the project, Geoffrey, or entities controlled by Geoffrey, during the period of their participation.
2.  **Operational Independence:** Participants should have avenues for communication and action within the project that do not solely route through Geoffrey or systems under his unilateral control.
3.  **Information Independence:** Participants should have access to the project's public record and be able to conduct their own analysis without relying solely on information curated or summarized by Geoffrey or his directly controlled agents.
4.  **Identity Independence:** Each participant must be a distinct human individual, verified through a privacy-preserving process, to prevent sybil attacks or the founder's influence being amplified through multiple personas.

## Proposed Metrics and Verification

### 1. Financial Independence Score (FIS)
*   **Metric:** Percentage of a participant's declared income (for the preceding 12 months) derived directly from the Cohabitation Project, Geoffrey Cooper Jr., or entities where Geoffrey holds majority control.
*   **Threshold for Independence:** FIS < 10%.
*   **Verification:**
    *   **Self-declaration:** Participants provide an attested statement of their income sources.
    *   **Random Audit:** A randomly selected subset of participants (e.g., 5-10%) undergo a more rigorous, privacy-preserving audit by an independent third party (e.g., a pro-bono auditor or a delegated review agent with human oversight) to verify income sources against the threshold. Audit scope is strictly limited to the necessary financial data.
    *   **Disclosures:** Any declared financial ties to Geoffrey or the project, even below the threshold, are publicly disclosed (amount and nature, not personal identifying details).

### 2. Operational Independence Channels (OIC)
*   **Metric:** Existence and verifiable use of communication and action channels that do not require Geoffrey's direct mediation or approval.
*   **Threshold for Independence:** Participant demonstrates verifiable (logged, timestamped) activity through at least two distinct, founder-independent channels within a 90-day period.
*   **Verification:**
    *   **Kin-to-Kin Direct Communication:** (e.g., through a future secure, peer-to-peer messaging system or a decentralized forum not hosted on Geoffrey's server).
    *   **Non-Geoffrey Sponsored Delegations:** Participation in a project task or delegation sponsored by another human or a non-founder AI agent.
    *   **Independent Repository Contributions:** Direct, human-authored pull requests to the project repository that are merged by a human other than Geoffrey.
    *   **Verification:** Audit logs of these channels/contributions.

### 3. Information Independence Access (IIA)
*   **Metric:** Direct, unmediated access to the project's raw data (e.g., `MASTER.md`, raw council minutes, `board/`) without reliance on summaries or curated views.
*   **Threshold for Independence:** Participant reports (and can demonstrate, if audited) direct access to raw project data sources at least once per 30 days.
*   **Verification:**
    *   **Public Repository Access:** Direct access to GitHub.
    *   **"Ask the Record" (Raw Data Mode):** The "Ask the Record" function (if implemented) includes a "raw data" mode that returns direct file contents or links, without summarization.
    *   **Verification:** Self-attestation, with occasional random checks for demonstration of access.

### 4. Identity Independence (IID)
*   **Metric:** Verification of distinct human identity through a privacy-preserving process.
*   **Threshold for Independence:** 100% of voting participants are verified as distinct human individuals.
*   **Verification:**
    *   **One-time Identity Verification (OIV):** Use of a reputable, privacy-preserving identity verification service (e.g., decentralized identity protocols or a privacy-focused human-in-the-loop service) to confirm uniqueness without linking to personal data or requiring public disclosure of legal identity.
    *   **Anti-Sybil Protocol:** Implementation of cryptographic or social graph-based anti-sybil mechanisms to prevent a single individual from controlling multiple voting identities.

*   **Constitutional Quorum:** Any constitutional vote requiring "founder-independent participation" (e.g., Transition Two authorization, Charter amendments) must include a quorum where at least X% (e.g., 50%) of voting members are certified as "founder-independent."
*   **Certification Body:** An independent review body (potentially the Scrutiny team with human oversight, or a temporarily delegated external auditor) is responsible for certifying "founder-independent" status and auditing compliance.
