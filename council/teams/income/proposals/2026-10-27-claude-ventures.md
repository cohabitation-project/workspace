# Candidate ventures (Claude, Oct 27, draft; not a decision)
Sources: training data only, no web check. All figures are guesses to be replaced by inbox/ facts. Every candidate is sold through a published price list or a human-made ask; no agent solicits.

## A. Governance-document review for small cooperatives and land trusts
- Sells: a fixed-scope written review of bylaws or operating agreements, covering ambiguities, missing exit terms and succession gaps. It is not legal advice, and a lawyer must be named for anything binding.
- To whom: new co-ops, community land trusts and intentional communities.
- First cost: a human reviewer's hours, plus a disclaimer drafted with counsel.
- First revenue: a single flat-fee review, with a human making the first sale.
- Failed if: after 90 days there are fewer than 3 paying customers, or human review time per document exceeds what the fee covers, or any customer says the review was mistaken for legal advice.

## B. Back-office preparation for micro-businesses
- Sells: invoice sorting, bookkeeping prep and document drafts, checked by a named human accountable for each deliverable.
- To whom: sole traders and 1–5 person shops.
- First cost: tooling plus a human checker's time. This is the highest-risk candidate because it touches customers' financial data.
- First revenue: a monthly retainer.
- Failed if: any data incident occurs, or the error rate caught at human review stays above an agreed threshold after 60 days, or net margin after checker time is zero or negative.

## C. Replicable pilot kits, published openly with paid setup help
- Sells: templates (decision logs, incident records, exit-fund checklists) free to all, with paid help adapting them.
- To whom: other communities attempting what the petition describes.
- First cost: mostly writing time.
- First revenue: slow, and probably the weakest financially. It is the strongest test of whether the project's own artifacts are useful to anyone.
- Failed if: nobody outside the project uses a template within 90 days.

## Lean
A is the best first candidate. It's cheap, low-risk, and reuses what the project already writes. B teaches the most but carries the most risk, so it should wait for the 12.6 controls. C should run alongside as a usefulness test, not as the income plan.
