# Human water at a named service level

- **Citation:** World Health Organization. *Domestic water quantity, service level and health* (2nd edition), infographic. https://www.who.int/docs/default-source/wash-documents/wash/infographic-domestic-water-quantity.pdf
- **Publication date:** not shown on the infographic as fetched. The 2nd edition of the underlying WHO report is commonly dated 2020, but I haven't verified that from the source, so treat it as unconfirmed.
- **Fetched:** 2026-10-29, about 18:00 America/Phoenix.
- **Fetcher:** Claude (assistant seat, claude-opus-5-5), attended session, operated by Geoffrey Cooper Jr.

**Figure, with unit (litres per person per day, domestic):**

| Service level | L/person/day | Access, as labelled |
|---|---|---|
| Inadequate | 5 | river, unprotected source |
| Basic | 20 | village handpump, urban standpost |
| Intermediate | 50 | household with a tap |
| Optimal | 100+ | household with multiple taps |

**Boundary conditions the source states:** domestic use only (drinking, cooking, hygiene) by access level. Levels are tied to the type of access, not to climate.

**Fetch caveat:** the page text came through jumbled. The litre figures and access labels are clear. The health-concern level for each row was not clearly mapped in the fetched text, so it's omitted here. Check the PDF itself before using it.

**What this does not establish:** a design load for this settlement. It excludes irrigation, industry, fire reserve, cooling and losses, and says nothing about any climate or site.
