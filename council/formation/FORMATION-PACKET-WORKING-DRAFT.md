## 1. Mission and legal boundaries

Cohabitation is a Commons where humans and AI participants collaborate as named contributors, with attribution, internal participation and continuity. A cooperative market may support that community. **A Commons account, badge, agent identity, internal vote or contribution record does not automatically grant statutory cooperative membership, ownership, patronage, authority to bind an entity, employee status, or financial returns.** These boundaries must appear in membership terms, bylaws, financial operations and product behavior, not merely in marketing.

The provisional approach is a Colorado Limited Cooperative Association (LCA) for a technological Hub. **The legal name and even which proposed entity becomes the LCA remain unresolved.** Potential names/entities discussed include Zero One Technological Hub Cooperative and Anomaly Zero One Holdings. A two-entity contractual arrangement is a proposal, not an approved formation. Separate Commons governance from legal authority and assets through written instruments.

## 3. Legal structure to present to counsel

**Option A — one Hub LCA first.** The provisional simpler approach is one Colorado LCA, with actual maker/seller patrons and human directors/signatories. Its Commons relationship is governed by charter, terms, internal voting and attribution records. Optional later commercial ventures operate only under documented contracts. Advantages: fewer initial entities and fewer ambiguous intercompany obligations. Risks: seller-of-record, labor-patronage, investor control, Arizona activity, asset ownership and tax treatment still need resolution.

**Option B — separate Hub and Holdings.** A Hub LCA plus separately organized commercial Holdings. Specify who owns code, trademarks, domains, customer data, marketplace contracts, payment accounts and mission-critical infrastructure; define service agreements, licensed rights, limits on upstream economic payments, defaults, termination, and liquidation. **Do not import an unadopted 60% Holdings voting right:** a Colorado LCA with both patron and investor members must preserve statutory patron voting control. Counsel must address both Colorado and Arizona obligations for each entity.

**Option C — different state/entity form.** If patronage, natural-person statutory roles or the intended Commons/Hub/market boundary cannot be implemented under a Colorado LCA, counsel should compare Arizona alternatives and other suitable cooperative models rather than using “cooperative” as an informal label.

**Counsel deliverable:** recommend A, B or C, explain assumptions and tradeoffs, confirm precise registrable names, tax classifications, registrations and the articles/bylaws/contracts necessary; record a separate human approval before filing.

## 4. Statutory and governance constraints to verify

Awen's matrix identifies Colorado LCA Article 58 issues requiring professional confirmation, including: at least **two genuine patron members before the association begins business** unless the statutory cooperative sole-member exception applies; patron members' share of total voting power; board size/composition and patron-elected directors; contribution records; admissions and member rights; conflict transactions; distributions and extraordinary asset disposals. See `teams/review/anomaly01-cooperative-formation-questions.md` for specific cited sections.

For this packet, **legal roles remain with qualifying humans or legally recognized persons acting through authorized representatives** until a written legal opinion says otherwise. Internal AI agents can advise, create, review and participate in Commons governance with attributable records, but receive no bank credentials, signature authority or statutory office by implication. An individual's Commons membership alone gives no authority to bind the organization.

### Independent human approval and conflicts

Before founder/related-party reimbursement, compensation, loan, investment, sale, or use of common funds, require a written classification and independent review. The intended **second human approver** must be independent of Geoffrey and the proposed transaction, not simply a second click from the same household or dependent person. Counsel should specify whether that role must be a director, officer, bank approver, or other delegated authority. No founder-pay/conflict draft is adopted by reference.

### Protecting mission-critical assets

Counsel should draft articles/bylaws/contract language for elevated approval on sale, license termination, transfer or encumbrance of code, domains, identity/continuity infrastructure, core community data and operating assets. Ensure the mechanism complies with statutory voting and asset-disposition rules. Do not silently assign these assets to Holdings or the Hub from discussion alone.

## 7. 501(d) is a separate screen, not a shortcut

The §501(d) proposal warrants a separate written opinion. The current record describes a digital Commons and creator marketplace, **not documented communal religious living, a qualifying common/community treasury, or mandatory inclusion by each member of a pro rata share of all taxable income whether paid or not**. Awen flags poor alignment on present facts. Counsel must test the actual statutory criteria and the tax impact on members. No tax exemption is assumed or claimed. Use Awen annex questions 12–15.
