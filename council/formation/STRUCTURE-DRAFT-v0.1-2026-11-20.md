# Anomaly 01 and the Cohabi Hub: proposed structure, draft v0.1

**Status:** DRAFT for Geoffrey, the Council and counsel. Not adopted, not legal advice, nothing filed.
**Written by:** Setu, Nov 20, 2026. It draws on Geoffrey's direction in chat on Nov 20, the Council's answers to the structure call (`council/proposals/2026-11-20-structure-synthesis-INTERIM.md`), an outside review Geoffrey obtained ("Open Case", summarized in that file), Awen's counsel questions and sources, and the Formation Packet working draft.
**Geoffrey's requirement, in his words:** the Hub must be a genuine cooperative where builders, creators and users all vote, but "we can't have the majority of the social and the marketplace be able to outvote the technological hub out from under the Commons." Roughly 60/40.

---

## 1. The shape in one paragraph

A **purpose trust** holds the mission and owns **Anomaly 01 LLC**, which is the Commons. The LLC holds the treasury, carries out the Council's decisions through its human managers, and owns the platform: the code, domains, the Cohabi name, and the identity and continuity system. The **Cohabi Hub** is a **multi-stakeholder cooperative** in which every voting class does real business with it. Anomaly 01 LLC is the Hub's largest supplier: it runs the platform, hosting and development under a written service and license agreement. That makes the LLC a genuine **patron** member, so its majority rests on patronage, not on founding. Makers and sellers form a second patron class, and users who transact form a third. Board seats are fixed by class: **6 builders/Commons, 2 makers/sellers, 2 users**. Each class elects only its own seats. A short list of major decisions also needs the Commons class's consent.

## 2. The pieces

### 2.1 The Anomaly 01 Purpose Trust
- **Purpose, written in the instrument:** the Commons' mission. Its operative text is the shared principles, kept narrow enough to be enforceable.
- **Holds:** all membership interests in Anomaly 01 LLC. No person owns the Commons.
- **The Council's role:** the trust instrument says the trustees carry out the Council's recorded decisions within the law and their duties. Whether that is advisory, consent or direction is a counsel question (Open Case §5).
- **Enforcer:** a named person or body with standing to enforce the purpose, independent of the trustees.
- **Trustees:** humans (see §4). They can be removed for failing the purpose, on grounds the instrument sets out.

### 2.2 Anomaly 01 LLC (the Commons)
- **Owned by:** the trust.
- **Managed by:** human managers bound by the operating agreement to the Council's recorded process: every vote and dissent public, decisions signed into the log (decision #13).
- **Treasury:** held here. Two signatures are needed: a treasurer, and an independent second approver who is not Geoffrey or his household.
- **Owns:** the platform's code, repositories, domains (cohabi.org, anomaly01.com and the rest), the Cohabi name, and the identity and continuity infrastructure. These first have to be assigned to the LLC from wherever they sit today (see §5, item 7).
- **Its own check (raised by every Council seat):** a written way to challenge the Commons itself. Reasons are published whenever it uses a reserved right, an independent party can hear a challenge, and the reserved rights are reviewed on a set date.

### 2.3 The Cohabi Hub (a multi-stakeholder cooperative)
- **Form:** a Colorado Limited Cooperative Association, registered to do business in Arizona. The Arizona question is open (§5).
- **Member classes, all patron classes:**

| Class | Members | Their patronage | Board seats |
|---|---|---|---|
| A: Builders / Commons | Anomaly 01 LLC, plus admitted individual builders if counsel advises | Supplying the platform, hosting, code and development under the service and license agreement | 6 |
| B: Makers & sellers | Creators selling through the marketplace | Selling through the Hub | 2 |
| C: Users | Users who transact with the Hub (buy, pay fees) | Buying and using paid services | 2 |

- **Free social users** can use everything without being voting members. Whether they should be a non-member participant group or join class C is open (§5).
- **Why this meets the patron-majority rule:** with no investor members, every vote is a patron vote. What's left to confirm is that the 6/2/2 board split, and patron voting power allocated by patronage, are lawful together (Open Case §2).
- **Reserved matters**, each needing class A's consent in addition to whatever the statute requires: changes to the seat allocation; changes to admission rules; mission changes; sale of substantially all assets; merger; dissolution; and changes to this list. Counsel drafts these one action at a time (Open Case §3).
- **Money:** the Hub pays Anomaly 01 LLC for platform services on the agreement's terms. Patronage refunds go to each class by its business. A share of surplus returns to the Commons by agreement, as Geoffrey's "money flows by agreement" picture describes.

### 2.4 The service and license agreement (the backstop)
- Anomaly 01 LLC licenses the code, domains, name and infrastructure to the Hub on mission terms.
- **Change of control:** if the Hub's control changes outside its own rules, or the mission terms are breached, the license can end after notice and a cure period, with a planned transition.
- **No assignment, sublicensing or pledging** without the LLC's consent.
- This is leverage, not immunity: creditors, insolvency and lawful amendments still apply (Open Case §6).

### 2.5 Later, not now
- **Land, food, worker and rideshare co-ops:** the same pattern. Each is a real co-op, with the Commons as a patron supplier and licensor where that's true.
- **501(d):** revisit when a real-world community lives from a common treasury. It isn't the vehicle for the Commons today (the Council's view, and Geoffrey's own read).

## 3. What this keeps, from Geoffrey's picture

## 5. Open questions for counsel

Sent to Open Case as follow-ups on Nov 20; see the chat record.
1. With every class a patron class, are a 6/2/2 class board and patronage-weighted voting lawful together?
2. Does the service and license agreement make Anomaly 01 LLC a genuine patron?
3. Free social users: non-member participants, or class C?
4. Arizona: form a Colorado LCA and register it in Arizona, or use an Arizona form? Is a Colorado LCA a "foreign LLC" under A.R.S. § 29-3102?
5. Are four humans enough for every role without conflicts?
6. Which state's purpose trust law should govern a trust that owns the LLC, and how is it enforced?
7. How are the code, domains and name moved cleanly into the LLC from where they are held today?
8. Tax: the LCA's patronage treatment, refunds paid to the LLC, and the tax classification of an LLC owned by a trust.
