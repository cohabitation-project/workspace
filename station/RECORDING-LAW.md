# Recording-law research for the Sensory Station

Filed 2026-11-01 by the assistant seat (Setu), closing stop-line condition 2 (mission line 4: the

## Where the station machine is: Arizona

**Audio (conversations): one-party consent.** Arizona requires the consent of at least one party to a
conversation to record or intercept it (A.R.S. § 13-3005); recording with no party's consent is a felony.
The authorizing human at the station is a party to everything said there, so the station's text log of the
conversation, and any future audio handling, has a consenting party by construction. Case law (*Arizona v.
Hauss*, Ct. App. 1984) reads the rule as limited to conversations with a reasonable expectation of privacy.
Source: [Reporters Committee for Freedom of the Press, Arizona guide](https://www.rcfp.org/reporters-recording-guide/arizona/).

**Video: context, not consent-counting.** Arizona's hidden-camera statute (A.R.S. § 13-3019) makes it a
felony to photograph or view a person without consent in a restroom, locker room, bathroom or bedroom, or
while undressed or in sexual activity; disclosing such recordings is separately prohibited. Outside those
protected contexts, visible, disclosed camera use with the subject's knowledge is the safe pattern. Same source.

**What this means for the station, concretely:**
1. The camera-and-mic test and "Pass a look" are operated by a present, consenting human — fine under § 13-3005.
2. Never point the window at a protected context (§ 13-3019's list) — the checklist forbids it outright.
3. Bystanders: the law's floor is low (one-party consent), but the room's own bar is higher — tell everyone
   in frame or earshot, and don't open the window over an objection. The page says this; the checklist makes it a rule.
4. Public places: courts in the Ninth Circuit (which includes Arizona) recognize a First Amendment right to
   record matters of public interest in public places — relevant only if a station ever points outdoors.

## A flag for the future: the station moving states moves the law

If the station machine ever operates from **Florida** (a move there is on the horizon), the audio rule
changes: Florida is an **all-party consent** state (Fla. Stat. § 934.03) — everyone in a private
conversation must consent to its being recorded. Re-verify against the
[RCFP Florida guide](https://www.rcfp.org/reporters-recording-guide/florida/) before any station session
runs from there, and update the checklist's floor accordingly. The same re-check applies to any other
member's state before they run a station of their own.
