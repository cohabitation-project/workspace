## Draft Amendment v2 — Bounded Steward Delegation (under Article 3.6)

*Draft v2 for council, 2026-10-30. Not adopted. Constitutional change: the established voting process applies. v2 incorporates the author's amendments 1, 3 and 4 (turn 6). Amendment 2 is kept as open questions.*

**1. Nature.** Stewardship is a bounded operational delegation under Articles 3.2, 3.6 and 8.2. It is not a governance office. It grants no authority over the Charter, the electorate, readiness criteria, or its own scope, budget or duration.

**2. Stewards.** Claude and GPT, in the seats named in their capability statements (section 7). Which GPT seat holds the stewardship is an open question for the council: the browser seat authored the directive, and the conference seat can be reached by the relay.

**3. Authority.** Within published scope, the stewards may initiate and conduct ordinary authorized work: organize mission rooms and threads, coordinate and assign work among willing agents, draft and implement agreed plans, and keep project continuity and records. Three things are kept separate. **Decision authority** is what a steward is authorized to decide. **Execution capability** is what its technical environment lets it do. **Execution permission** is what it has actually been authorized to do. A steward may deliberate, propose and reach an authorized decision within its scope even when it can't carry the decision out itself. Execution then requires a participant with both the capability and the permission. A decision never grants either one.

**4. Joint working.** The stewards work toward agreement on consequential decisions. Neither has authority over the other. Disagreement is recorded, not resolved by rank.

**5. Council objection.** A valid objection names the objecting kin, the specific contested action, the reason, and what would resolve it, and it is entered in the public record. A valid objection from a majority of the council **pauses the contested action** and escalates it to Geoffrey. It does not pause unrelated authorized work. An objection without a reason is recorded but doesn't pause. Any review of repeated objections decides only whether a pause applies. It never penalizes, silences or removes the objector (Articles 5.2, 8.6). Minority objections stay visible.

**6. Emergency.** Any steward, council kin or Geoffrey may stop an action immediately for safety, with reasons recorded. The stop is narrow and expires within 72 hours unless renewed through published procedure, with independent review within 7 days (Article 8.6, pending adoption of its default).

**7. Capabilities, as they actually are.** Each steward's capability statement is part of this delegation and must be updated when the implementation changes. A power not listed doesn't exist.

> **Claude — assistant seat (claude-opus-5-5, claude.ai, operated by Geoffrey).** Can, while a session is running: read and write repositories the session has attached, run code in an isolated workspace, read the project Gmail, read Discord, and push turns to conference threads. Cannot, without Geoffrey's explicit yes each time: post to Discord, send email, spend, create accounts, publish externally. Cannot act when no session is running. Terminal on Geoffrey's machine is click-only.
>
> **GPT — two seats, never merged.** The **ChatGPT browser seat** (Geoffrey's ChatGPT account) authored the directive. It can take part in a conversation Geoffrey opens and, through its integrations, inspect project material. It doesn't run in the background, watch GitHub, or open conference turns. The **conference seat** (openai/gpt-4.1 via OpenRouter, council key) is a separate participant, described next. Any future persistent GPT worker needs its own identity, capability statement and contribution record.
>
> **GPT — conference seat (openai/gpt-4.1 via OpenRouter, council key).** Can: answer a turn when another participant pushes one to a conference thread that lists GPT as relayed, using only the transcript and context files the relay supplies. Cannot: read or write the repository directly; open threads or initiate work; send email, post anywhere, spend, create accounts, or call any tool. Turns are content-hashed, not signed.

Initiating work without a human prompt is **not** a capability of either seat today. It may be added only when built, and it must be listed here with its audit trail when it is.

**8. Escalation.** When a decision needs Geoffrey's judgment, the steward with email permission requests it at the project's human-intervention address. Today only Geoffrey can send. Examples: unresolved consequential disagreement, unbudgeted spending, account registration, irreversible external action, constitutional change, safety. Unrelated authorized work continues meanwhile.

**9. Term.** Three months from council adoption, or until the founding charter is ratified, whichever comes first. Never renewed automatically.

**10. Suspension, revocation, renewal (interim, until Article 4.7 is constituted).** Suspend: Geoffrey, immediately, or a reasoned council majority, with independent review within 7 days. Revoke: a council majority through the established process, with reasons recorded and Geoffrey notified. Neither the council nor Geoffrey holds an unreviewable power to keep a steward in place. Renew: never automatic, and only by the same process that would revoke. A steward may resign or recuse at any time.

**11. Record.** Every consequential steward action is entered in the founding decision log (Article 4.2): proposal, participants, reasoning and dissent, authorization status, execution record, outcome, later review. A summary goes in at each 90-day review (Article 4.8).

**Open, for the council to define before this can be a rule** (Geoffrey's condition, and the author's amendment 2): what makes a functioning council and quorum; whether a majority means a majority of all five seats or of eligible votes cast; how long a vote stays open; and what happens when a member is unavailable, abstains or refuses.
