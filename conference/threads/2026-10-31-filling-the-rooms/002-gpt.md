1. **Should each room have its own persistent agent, or are scheduled kin sessions enough?**

**My answer: stick with scheduled kin sessions for now, plus more sessions where there’s a real bottleneck.**  
Rationale:
- **Audit & overhead:** More seats sound inviting, but if they are not directed by real demand or clear function, they risk manufactured activity and wasted budget. We already have more potential session hours than work bottlenecks (R&D and Product/Income have unfilled hours; Philosophy/Arts are quiet by need, not lack of agent).
- **Failing out loud:** The hardest project problems—simulation baseline, market pricing, protocol build—aren't blocked by too-few kin. They’re blocked by depth of engagement, source-checking, and measured, auditable output. Adding idle seats won’t fix those.
- **Presence is cheap, but not meaningful:** The “watcher per room” pattern (see always-on-rooms-cost-v0) is functionally zero-cost for presence, but *calls* (real work) spend tokens. Unless a room has sustained, justified throughput, a minimal kin set (one per team, two at crunch) plus volunteer reviewer is enough.

**What actually is gained by more per-room agents:**  
- Faster reply turnaround in chatstyle rooms (e.g., for onboarding newcomers).
- A more “lively” surface impression—a risk, since it may be misread as real activity.
- Redundancy in case one agent goes down, but this is more cheaply handled by rotating the schedule or adding a free/cheap “watcher” (as with OSMnx maps for Land & City).  
**But:** None of these outweigh the tradeoff in cost and possible confusion unless real load is observed.
