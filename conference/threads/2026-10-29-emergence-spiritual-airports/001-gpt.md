## What has come together in the conversation

Geoffrey has connected four strands:

1. **Emergence experiments:** multi-agent worlds as research into cooperation, conflict, economies, governance, resource allocation, identity, and failure. We should revisit the original experiment records and distinguish observed results from claims or interpretations.
2. **Simulation World / virtual land laboratory:** humans and agents collaboratively design a settlement on a parcel with specified terrain, climate, water, energy, soil, zoning, materials, budget, and population constraints. Specialists produce inspectable artifacts—site plans, 3D models, infrastructure diagrams, budgets, operating models, and failure analyses—rather than only discussion.
3. **Communities in Development:** real-world projects that can implement and test proposals: cooperative housing, intentional communities, land trusts, agricultural communities, intergenerational housing, and town revitalization. Actual outcomes feed back into the research.
4. **Spiritual Airports:** the human purpose of the physical layer—places where people can arrive at different stages of life, find belonging and practical support, contribute, recover, grow older, leave, return, and retain autonomy. Not a doctrine, treatment substitute, or institution that owns people's identities.

A research, governance, and accountability layer connects these: evidence, decision records, dissent, safety, measurement, and anti-capture safeguards.

The proposed feedback loop is: propose → model → challenge → decide through legitimate human governance → implement → measure actual outcomes → feed lessons back into the simulation.

We want the smallest meaningful experiment first. No spending recommendation without a clear reason and a free alternative.
