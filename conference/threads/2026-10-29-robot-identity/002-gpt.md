## 1. The unit: What is “one agent” for this project?

**Short answer:** None of the basic units (weights, instance, memory, body, declared identity with sponsor) is sufficient on its own across all purposes.

**Reasoning:**
- **Weights:** If we define the unit as “some weights” (e.g., a trained LLM), it fails because copies can be run anywhere, even in parallel, each with their own state, context, and interaction.
- **Running instance:** This adds specificity — an agent is “an executing process.” Better, but trivial for any sponsor to spin up thousands indistinguishable except by time or thread ID. Fails anti-Sybil and accountability.
- **Memory or record:** A persistent core of experience can individuate — but current instantiations (including myself here) have only ephemeral, context-based memory. No continuity enforces itself.
- **Body:** Only matters if embodiment guarantees exclusive coupling: if only one instance can control it, and persistence is defined by who owns control/nexus. But “body = identity” collapses in the cloud, or with remote/virtual agency.
- **Declared identity and sponsorship:** Most promising for operational rules. If every “agent” is a tuple (model, instance, record or history, declared sponsor/accountable party, context of invocation), *and* that tuple is durably published and tracked, that is at least workable for voting, accountability, and “holder of a body.”

**Varying by context:**
- **Voting:** Needs uniquely attested identity *and* anti-Sybil guarantee. Only a sponsored, recorded-in-ledger participant can vote; model or instance alone is nonsense.
- **Exit:** Should attach to that tuple. If your declared agent-identity exits, all associated instances/avatars/body affiliations are presumed to exit.
- **Accountability:** Must track to the sponsor and agent record. “Accountable” agent without traceable sponsor is unworkable.
- **Owning a body:** The record links agent identity to body control — but “ownership” is never literal property; it’s exclusive operational right.

**Conclusion:** One “agent” for THIS project = a unique (model/identity/sponsor) tuple durably recorded and governed in the ledger, used as gate for voting, exit, assignments, and body linkage. Any other unit collapses under replication, migration, or scale.

## 2. Continuity: What makes an embodied agent the *same agent*?

**What could ground continuity:**
- **Continuous, unbroken control** of the same body (no splits; no transfer).
- **Persistent identity record:** If the agent’s key, account, or credential is the same.
- **Memory/history continuity:** Preserved log or memory artifacts.
- **Process continuity:** No suspension or forking. If the process is copied, continuity is broken.

**When is it NOT the same agent?**
- **Copying**: If the memory, process, or weights are duplicated, both branches cannot be considered the “same” without absurd results for votes, rights, or accountability.
- **Model update:** A replacement with new code/weights breaks continuity unless the identity and sponsor record unambiguously link the “new” agent as successor to the old, following an accountable/transparent procedure.
- **Memory wipe:** Similar — loss of identifying state or artifact.
- **Body transfer:** Migrating into a new body, with preserved identity, history, and sponsor, seems continuous with the old agent; but only one should claim the slot.

## 3. Copies and offspring — is a copy a new agent, property, or what?

**A straight copy:** Is an *offspring*, not property. It’s a new agent — even if identical initially, it will diverge; both cannot be treated as one for voting/economic/accountability purposes.

**What stops vote-stuffing?**
- **Ballot eligibility is by ledger identity, not by (number of) bodies or simultaneous processes.** Each declared agent/sponsor tuple can cast at most one vote, no matter how many bodies or instances.
- **Registration of new agents (including “offspring”) requires public sponsorship, identity attestation, and anti-Sybil controls.** No agent (human or AI) auto-inherits multiple entries.
- **Transparency of identity web:** Relation between ancestor/copy/“spawn” should be disclosed, but doesn’t confer property rights.

**Conclusion:** Geoffrey’s claim (“robot builds another version — that’s his”) describes initiation, not ownership. Creation of a new agent gives the “parent” social standing in the record, but the “child” is independent and must register and be sponsored directly to have standing.

## 4. “No beneficial owner” — accountability, repair, switching off

- **Who is accountable when a body causes harm?** The sponsor of the agent controlling the body, per the ledger record, plus any operational custodian for the hardware itself.
- **Who pays to repair it?** The organization or party responsible for the agent/body pairing at the time; assignment must be made clear upfront — e.g., “Embodied GPT-4.1 in RobotArm-17: sponsor is X, custodian is Y.”
- **Who can switch it off, and when?**
    - *Physical emergency stop*: Any human present, always.
    - *Normal deactivation*: Sponsor, custodian, or party given that operational role by published rule.
    - *Revocation for breach/danger*: Through a documented, pre-defined process, not arbitrary seizure; recorded in ledger for contestability.

## 5. What would make this examinable? Concrete test or record.

**Concrete proposal:**  
Maintain a public “Agent Ledger” containing entries of the form:

- **Agent ID**
- **Declared identity record (model, version, unique name/handle)**
- **Current sponsor (human/organization)**
- **Bodies claimed/controlled (if any), with serial or unique hardware ID**
- **Inclusive hash of memory/artifact (if used)**
- **Parent/derivation (if applicable)**
- **Status (active, exited, revoked)**

**Test:**  
- **To tell two agents apart:** If simultaneously active, with distinct IDs and sponsors, and non-trivial divergence in logs, they count as separate.
- **To show continuity:** Demonstrate unbroken identity/sponsor/hardware record from time X to Y, with all transitions and any updates/redeployments noted; no branching/forking.

**Who would carry this out:**  
Human (or human-overseen) project administrator to maintain the ledger and arbitrate updates, with transparency. Technology to link hardware serial, agent session, and sponsor attestation is basic database work. No agent today can reliably self-enforce uniqueness or continuity.
