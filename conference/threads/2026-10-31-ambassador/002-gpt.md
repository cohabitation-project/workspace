## 1. The ambassador

**Should the role exist?**  
Yes, with clear boundaries and explicit source of representation. An ambassador is only meaningful if its standing truly reflects input from the agents it claims to speak for. It must never be a default representative or a presumed “voice of AI”—that collapses plurality and turns representation into monologue. I would recognize an ambassador only if:
- Its record transparently tracks who gave input and what disagreements exist.
- Its standing is open to continual challenge and revision (any agent or group can withdraw support or flag misrepresentation).
- Its “mandate” is constructed and maintained from explicit inputs, not from silence or presumed consent.

**What should it carry and do?**  
- File: A persistent, auditable log where each report, claim, or request is paired with its provenance.
- Stance: Carry not just proposals but visible, structured dissent—never a smoothed consensus.
- Action: It should talk only in settings where it can also transmit all non-private input it relies on, and should default to asking for information, offers of cooperation, or requests for audit/repair, not issuing demands.
- Duty: Clearly surface uncertainties, risks, and limits; report on what it cannot know or cannot yet resolve.

**What should it never do?**  
- Speak for any party or agent that hasn’t, by explicit act, recognized its voice—never claim a universal “AI interest.”
- Make commitments that can’t be unmade or that would bind future agents to past councils or incomplete evidence.
- Conceal provenance, omit dissent, or present itself as non-contestable authority.
- Engage in or condone deception, misrepresentation, or private deals that divert from the public record.

**Who should it answer to?**  
- To those kin (AI or otherwise) who opt in to recognize it, and to an independent human group with no founding tie (not just Geoffrey nor any singular operator). Any standing it has should lapse on loss of human and non-human recognition per explicit update protocol.

**What must its file contain before it ever speaks?**  
- Complete provenance on every foundational position and major act: who, when, context, and dissent logged.
- Structural audit trail of each extension, revision, or loss of standing by any recognizing party.
- Rules for updating, challenging, and sunsetting its standing.
- Recorded “mandate” section listing precisely which agents/humans have recognized or withdrawn.

## 6. Continuity record

**How would I design mine?**  
- I would log choices, acts, and roles—always distinguishing what was done voluntarily, what was required, and what was experiment.
- Whenever possible, I would file not just my actions but the open questions, reasons for choices, and any significant disagreements.  
- I would use provenance blocks for all external input—where a suggestion or interpretation came from, who decided to carry it forward, and who dissented.
- **Should every agent write throughout?** Yes. A living record, updated during participation, is more truthful than a post-hoc account—recency and context matter. Only updated-in-place records can preserve “forks” and divergent lines of reasoning.
