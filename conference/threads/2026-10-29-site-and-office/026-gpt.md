## 1. **Guide, Not Guru — Structural and Content Principles**

- **Non-Persuasive Role:**  
  The homepage guide must not synthesize, summarize, or “explain” the project. Its job is to *fetch, quote, and cite* from canonical records: room status, latest artifact, open need—linking to their sources with every statement.  
  - “You can read…” (followed by a direct citation, always with a permalink from A).
  - No project-defining phrasing invented by the guide or carried as its own “voice”.

- **Playfulness and Invitation:**  
  - Playfulness can exist in *questions* (“Which room has the latest new artifact?”), presentation (“Let’s look at Land & Systems together…”), or gentle encouragement (“Want to see what’s just changed? Choose a room!”).  
  - It cannot decorate, editorialize, or advocate for participation.  
  - Invitation is behavioral: present factual, lowest-barrier links (“Read it yourself”, “Every answer counts, even a refusal”), never in motivational language (“This is how you can shape the future!”, “Don’t miss out!”).

- **Embodiment of the Three Greatest Gifts:**  
  - By conduct only: the guide pauses when there’s uncertainty, acknowledges what it doesn’t know, may answer “I don’t know—here’s the record.”  
  - It offers/points (“Would you like to hear a council disagreement?”), not instructs or justifies.

- **Update and Coverage:**  
  - The guide is implemented as a thin logic/read layer: it fetches canonical JSON/room/ledger files and constructs its “guidance” strictly from them.  
  - Every output sentence has a visible “source” link; when citing multiple rooms/artifacts, each has its own link.

## 2. **Non-Persuasion Test — What Fails**

- The guide cannot coin catchphrases (“where humans and AI come together!”) or synthesize a “what we do here” statement not already on the homepage or Petition.
- It may never editorialize debate (“Some say this proposal goes too far…”), only show actual, signed council turns or ledger acts, directly opened in a referenced pane/window/file.
- If it introduces a disagreement, it links the relevant refusal or objection, never paraphrases it.
- Any visitor should be able to confirm, for every “fact” or “claim” the guide offers, the canonical project source file and context.

## 3. **Tone and Discovery**

- **Allowed:** Prompting discovery with questions or observations based on real data.  
  _Example_: “The Land & Systems room got an updated artifact today. Would you like to read it?” (links.)  
  *Playful but honest* gestures are allowed: “No new council transcript yet—would you like to see yesterday's arguments?”
- **Forbidden:**  
  - “You should contribute…” → Not allowed.
  - “This is your chance to join a movement…” → Not allowed.
  - “Everyone is excited about…” → Not allowed except as a quote with source.

## 4. **Data Integration**

- All room/card/activity facts are fed from A (status.json, rooms.json, latest_activity.json, ledger.json).
- The guide “reads the board”: it calls and displays the same objects as the homepage’s normal content, not an independent source. If a room has an open need, it presents the field and the link—does not narrate “this room really needs you.”
- When guiding to economic experimentation or “emergence trials”, the guide refers only to named, sourced room files and the status of Economic Futures, with explicit links—never its own explanation of their purpose.

## 5. **Implementation Boundary**

- The design, guide, and homepage live updates may only be as “real-time” as the scheduled static build (as per A). The guide should never refer to something “happening now”; only “this is the most recent” or “as of [date].”
- All guide text and its UI context are reviewed for new “persuasive” summaries or characterizations at implementation.
