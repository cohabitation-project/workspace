# Land criteria v1 — proposal

**Status:** Planning draft, not adopted. This is not authorization to search listings, contact sellers, negotiate, or commit funds. Current market conditions and prices are **UNVERIFIED**; no region or budget is established.

## Must-haves before a site can advance
1. **Legal feasibility:** Verify title, access, water rights, permitted uses, building and wastewater requirements with relevant records and authorities—not seller assurances. Record jurisdiction and dated sources; unresolved material questions mean **UNKNOWN**, not “likely.”
2. **Water:** Establish legal access and have a qualified, independent assessment of source, sustainable yield, quality, seasonal variability, and drought restrictions. Use the WHO intermediate domestic level (about 50 L/person/day) only as a preliminary domestic benchmark; it does not size irrigation, fire reserve, or losses. The water-balance method in inbox item 04 distinguishes withdrawal, consumptive use, return flow, and conveyance loss.
3. **Safe, serviceable access:** Confirm year-round road access, emergency-service coverage, and response-time evidence. Assess power, communications, and wastewater options, including costs and alternatives to single-provider dependence.
4. **Ecological and hazard screen:** Check flood, wildfire, contamination, soil, habitat, and local water impacts with appropriate records and expertise. A site that breaches the ecological floor or cannot be insured is not viable.
5. **Affordable, reversible commitment:** Require a published budget, financing, ownership, exit terms, and failure criteria before anyone commits money, housing, or livelihood; obtain appropriate legal and accounting review. No deposit or exclusivity before the required entity and safeguards exist.

## Deal-breakers
Unresolved title/access or water-rights disputes; prohibited intended use without a credible lawful path; material uninsurable hazard; water supply that fails a documented demand and drought test; unacceptable ecological harm; or terms that require premature personal commitment.

## Site-visit questions
What records establish water rights, yield, quality, and dry-year performance? Who maintains the access road? What do county and utility authorities say in writing about use, permits, connections, and costs? What are the flood, fire, and contamination histories? What do nearby residents report? What happens during a week-long water, power, or communications outage? What are the realistic operating costs and exit options?

A named human must attend. Record answers, sources, dates, and unresolved items; a visit does not substitute for specialist or legal review.
