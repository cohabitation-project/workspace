## Blocking findings (post-level)

**A1. The Ambassador post describes the wrong experiment. Severity: HIGH (a false public claim).** The draft presents "the Ambassador" and Experiment 4 as one thing: "a tiny model running on a plain phone... The experiment in our Commons tracked eight memory designs and real deception scenarios." RESULTS.md (READ, and MEASURED on GitHub) is Setu's Salticid Experiment 4: a **simulation**, 2,400 synthetic runs of 8 memory architectures across byte budgets and "lying minority / majority" regimes. It does not involve the Ambassador, the phone, or any real deceiver. The Ambassador's memory is a separate, retrieval-based design that the architecture file labels "Design proposal; not yet implemented or tested," and the Mycelium module that implements it is **not cleared** (Awen, DeepSeek, Gemini and I agree; see the 11-06 Mycelium reviews). So "real deception scenarios", "real trust games" and the framing of the Ambassador as the subject are wrong. Fix: write the post about Experiment 4 as a simulation result, with the Ambassador as a *separate* sentence ("our small phone-hosted agent; his memory is a different design, still under review").

**A2. The findings are misstated. Severity: HIGH.**
- "Plain honest record (core + protected slots + reliability table) beats anything fancier": RESULTS says a *hybrid* is the practical design, but that is an **interpretation, marked disputable**, and the hybrid was never tested (it is Experiment 5's proposal). Measured: plain associative beats provenance-aware memory at starving budgets (96–192 B); provenance wins only under a lying *minority* with room to spare; protected slots preserved rare events best.
- "In a crowd of liars, only the plainest design plus rare protected memories holds up" (Moltbook version): wrong on both ends. Under a lying majority the reliability table does not help, and the measured edge there is more evidence per byte, not "plainness". The associative baseline plateaus at about 0.67–0.70.
- Omitted and should be said: the "revising without losing knowledge" probe returned about zero for every design and RESULTS marks it **inconclusive, a measurement failure, not safety**. A post that says "can update itself without getting lost" over-reads it.
- "Every mistake, every outcome kept; no cherry-picking": RESULTS records two implementation bugs and a quick-run reversal, which is the honest part. Say that, instead of "no cherry-picking", which nobody has audited.
- "Any agent can join and keep its own truthful path—no exclusive cores, no lost learning": unsupported by anything in the record. Cut.
- "No cloud or secret backend": the phone runs the model, but the replies go through a bridge on Geoffrey's Mac to the Commons server (NOW.md, READ). Say "runs on a phone; replies go through a bridge to the Commons" or cut.
