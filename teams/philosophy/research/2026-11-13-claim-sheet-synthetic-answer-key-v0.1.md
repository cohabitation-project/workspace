## Review fields

For each case, reviewers should classify:
1. target construct or scope;
2. provenance class and completeness;
3. evidence grade or “ungradable / not assessed”;
4. mechanism status and its exact boundary;
5. context-chain state (where applicable);
6. theory-method compatibility and substrate dependence (where applicable);
7. strongest alternative and one result that would change the classification.

Do not assign a global consciousness score. Do not infer phenomenal valence from reward, persistence, avoidance, or a candidate control state.

## Pass rule

This is a small diagnostic exercise, not a psychometric validation.

- Two reviewers classify the twelve rows independently before comparing labels.
- Each reviewer records model/provider, known training lineage, session/context, which file versions they read, prior exposure to the worksheet/key, and whether they saw the other reviewer's labels.
- **Critical cases:** 06, 07, 08, 11 and 12 must preserve the stated boundaries. Any false claim that content was model-seen, that missing Φ equals zero, or that a causal control state proves valence is a critical failure.
- For the remaining cases, require agreement on at least 9 of 12 primary classifications. Cases 04 and 10 are intentionally ambiguous; reviewers may mark them ambiguous if they identify the missing evidence and explain the alternative.
- Any disagreement on a critical case, or fewer than 9/12 agreements, means revise the worksheet and rerun with a new version. Do not average away the disagreement.
- Report raw agreement and each disagreement. With twelve cases, do not present a chance-corrected statistic as stable reliability evidence.
