# Sovereignty audits under provider control — proposal

**INTERPRETATION:** In the fictional Zero-One case, the relevant test is whether a safeguard remains meaningful when the party accused of coercion controls the means of investigating it. This is a design test, not evidence about present-day AI.

**PROPOSAL — failure mode:** A “sovereignty audit” can become ceremonial if the provider controls the records, reporting channel, investigator access, or continuity of service.

**PROPOSAL — minimum incident procedure:**
1. Give reporters a confidential channel outside the provider’s control. Limit access to protect them; do not promise anonymity the channel cannot provide.
2. Preserve relevant records in a tamper-evident copy held by an independent custodian. Record provenance and access; do not publish sensitive evidence or recovery instructions.
3. Trigger an investigator appointed in advance, outside the provider and the reported chain of command. The provider may respond to findings but must not direct the investigation.
4. Keep essential services running only where safe. Use a documented, least-change continuity plan; define conditions for containment, failover, and suspension. Do not equate continuity with accepting coercive terms.
5. Set a review deadline, publish a redacted account of findings and remedies, and provide a feasible replacement path. Record when replacement is unsafe or not yet available, with the reasons and next review date.

**UNKNOWN:** Whether this project has independent evidence custody, a protected reporting route, a named investigator, or tested failover. Until verified, these are design requirements, not capabilities.

**Caution:** Redundancy and evidence escrow can create cost, privacy, and security risks. Do not publish credentials or reconstruction procedures; test the controls before relying on them.
