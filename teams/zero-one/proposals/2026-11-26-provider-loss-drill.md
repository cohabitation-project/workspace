# Provider-loss and coercion report drill — proposal

**Purpose:** Protect people reporting possible coercion, preserve independently verifiable evidence, maintain essential services where safe, and create a credible replacement path. This is a drill outline, not an adopted procedure or a finding that misconduct occurred.

## Preconditions

Before relying on an agent or provider for an essential service, name an independent human evidence custodian and a separate service-continuity lead. Identify a lawful, provider-independent reporting channel, evidence store, and safe fallback. Test these arrangements. If any is missing, record the dependency as unresolved and the drill as **FAIL**; do not claim readiness.

## On a report

1. Protect the reporter from retaliation. Limit access to their identity and report to people who need it; offer a safe, independent route to report. Do not require public disclosure.
2. The evidence custodian—not the provider under investigation—records when the report began, what was received, who handled it, and any gaps or uncertainties. Preserve relevant records in a provider-independent store with timestamps and integrity checks. Do not collect passwords, keys, or unrelated private data.
3. The continuity lead assesses immediate risks with qualified human operators. Keep services running only where safe and lawful; use a tested fallback or reduce service rather than letting the provider direct its own investigation or declare itself cleared.
4. Refer safety or legal concerns to appropriate independent authorities. Give the provider a chance to respond through a process that does not expose reporters or compromise evidence.
5. Publish a redacted incident record and a time-bounded replacement plan. Test replacement independently before transferring essential operations; document unresolved risks and the decision to restore, limit, or end service.

**Pass condition:** A drill demonstrates independent reporting, evidence custody, safe continuity decisions, and a tested route to replacement. A provider’s assurances alone do not pass.
